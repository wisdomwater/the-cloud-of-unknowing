## 56 — The Mystery Beyond Every Thought

> *God is not the greatest thing we can imagine, but the One beyond imagination itself.*

The deeper the soul travels into contemplation, the more it discovers that God cannot be captured by any category of the mind.

At first this realization may feel unsettling. We naturally seek understanding. We want to describe God, define God, and organize our experiences of God into clear and meaningful frameworks. We find comfort in names, concepts, doctrines, and images. These are important gifts, and they often serve us well.

But eventually, love leads us beyond them.

The ancient writer carries this movement to its furthest edge. He reminds us that God is not merely beyond physical things. God is beyond the reach of the intellect itself. God is not an object among other objects that can be examined, measured, compared, or understood.

Even our highest thoughts remain too small.

God is not limited by any idea we have of wisdom.

God is not confined within our understanding of truth.

God is not contained within our concepts of goodness, unity, life, or being.

These words point toward Him, but they do not encompass Him.

This is why the contemplative tradition so often returns to humility.

The more deeply we encounter God, the less interested we become in possessing certainty. We begin to realize that mystery is not the enemy of faith. Mystery is one of faith's deepest companions. The soul gradually learns to rest in a reverent openness before a reality too vast to be mastered.

And yet this mystery is not distant.

It is intimate.

Closer than thought.

Closer than language.

Closer than the very awareness with which we seek Him.

The ancient writer even says that God is beyond both light and darkness, beyond truth and error. At first this sounds strange, but his meaning is simple. Whatever we understand by these words remains limited by human experience. God is not merely one thing among others that can be classified. God transcends every contrast by which the mind organizes reality.

God is not found on one side of our categories.

God is beyond the categories themselves.

This is why contemplative prayer eventually becomes so simple.

There comes a point where the soul no longer needs to fill the silence with explanations. It no longer feels compelled to grasp every mystery or resolve every question. Instead, it learns to remain present. It becomes willing to stand before the unknown without fear.

Not because it has all the answers.

But because it has discovered the One who holds all things.

The ancient writer reaches a remarkable conclusion. We cannot fully affirm God, because every affirmation is too small. We cannot fully deny God, because every denial is also too small. Every statement falls short.

God is beyond every yes.

God is beyond every no.

Beyond every description.

Beyond every definition.

Beyond every image.

Beyond every thought.

Yet somehow, in this very realization, the soul draws nearer.

For when we stop trying to fit God into the limits of our understanding, we become free to encounter Him as mystery. Love remains where concepts fail. Presence remains where explanations end. Communion remains where words fall silent.

This is the hidden wisdom of the contemplative path.

The goal is not to understand God completely.

The goal is to surrender more completely to God.

And in that surrender, the soul discovers a peace that does not depend upon certainty. It finds rest not in having mastered the mystery, but in being embraced by it.

For the One who surpasses all understanding is also the One who lovingly holds us even now.

### Breath Prayer

> **Inhale:** *Beyond all understanding*
>
> **Exhale:** *I rest in Your mystery*
