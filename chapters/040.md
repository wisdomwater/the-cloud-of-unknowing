## 40 — The Quiet Trust of Surrender

> *The soul grows steady when it learns to rest more in God than in its own striving.*

As love deepens within the soul, something begins to grow quieter inside. The endless need to analyze, defend, and manage every spiritual movement slowly loosens its grip. A deeper trust begins to take root — not a careless passivity, but a gentle confidence that God is nearer than fear and more faithful than the anxious mind can imagine.

The old writer says that when the soul becomes inwardly gathered into love, the “foot” of its love no longer stumbles so easily. In the beginning of contemplation, the mind is constantly pulled away by distractions, questions, fantasies, fears, and restless spiritual effort. Thoughts scatter the soul outward in many directions. But over time, as the heart learns to remain quietly turned toward God, there comes a steadiness that cannot be produced through force alone.

This path requires simplicity because the mind endlessly tries to pull us back into fragmentation. The moment we become trapped in endless mental searching, the soul loses its inward stillness. Contemplation is not the rejection of thought, but it is the refusal to let thought dominate the deepest movements of love. There is a kind of knowing that only appears when the mind softens its grip and allows the heart to remain quietly attentive to God beneath all words.

And slowly this prayer begins to spread into every part of life.

The writer speaks beautifully of carrying this inward awareness into everything: resting, working, speaking, eating, walking, silence, activity, labor, and stillness alike. Contemplation is not meant to remain sealed away inside isolated moments of prayer. It becomes a hidden orientation of the soul. The heart begins resting in God quietly beneath ordinary life itself.

Even fear begins to lose some of its power there.

The ancient contemplatives understood that deeper prayer often awakens resistance within us. Sometimes fear rises unexpectedly. Sometimes the mind becomes noisy, anxious, restless, or overwhelmed. Old wounds surface. Strange thoughts appear. The nervous system trembles as deeper layers of the self begin loosening their hold. The old writer described this in the symbolic language of spiritual attack, but beneath the imagery is a profoundly human truth: whenever the soul grows quieter, all the hidden noise within us becomes more noticeable for a time.

And yet the invitation remains the same.

Do not cling to the fear.

Do not abandon the quiet work of love.

Lean gently toward God instead.

The writer insists that divine Love remains nearer than the soul realizes in these moments. You are not being asked to preserve yourself through endless vigilance and strain. The contemplative path slowly teaches the soul how to release its exhausting self-protection and rest more fully in grace.

This surrender is not weakness.

It is trust ripened through love.

The soul that finally stops clutching itself so tightly often discovers, sometimes with great surprise, that it has been held by God all along.
