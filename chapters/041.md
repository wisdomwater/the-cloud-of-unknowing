## 41 — The Holy Rest Beyond Understanding

> *Love sometimes carries the soul beyond what reason can explain.*

There comes a place in the contemplative life where the mind can no longer lead the way.

The ancient writers spoke of this through the image of Rachel giving birth to Benjamin and passing away in the process. Rachel represented reason—the beautiful and necessary gift of understanding that helps guide us through the world. But Benjamin represented something beyond reason: the soul carried upward into the overflowing mystery of divine love.

And so there are moments when contemplation gently silences the need to explain everything. Not because reason is evil. Not because thought no longer matters. But because love sometimes draws the soul into a deeper knowing that words and analysis cannot fully contain.

When this happens, many people become frightened. The mind wants certainty. It wants to inspect, define, and organize what is happening within. It searches constantly for explanations, measurements, and proof. Yet the contemplative path invites you into another posture altogether—a quiet trust that God is present even when your understanding grows dim.

There is a humility in allowing mystery to remain mystery.

The soul does not lose itself in confusion, but in surrender. It ceases grasping long enough to rest in a Presence too vast for the intellect to master. This is why the mystics so often speak of darkness, silence, or unknowing. They are not praising ignorance. They are describing the holy tenderness that appears when the need for control begins to soften.

God has always touched souls suddenly and unexpectedly.

In earlier ages, some were seized by such love that they left behind ordinary concerns and gave themselves wholly to God without hesitation. And even now, in quieter times, the same grace still moves among human hearts. A person may be drawn inward without fully understanding why. A longing awakens. Prayer deepens. The noise of the world begins losing its grip. The soul slowly discovers that beneath all striving there is Another already holding it.

And strangely, the more a person entrusts themselves to this love, the more deeply they are carried. Not through force. Not through spiritual performance. But through surrender.

The contemplative life is not sustained by human strength alone. God Himself keeps the soul that rests in Him. The one who loosens their desperate need to preserve themselves through control often discovers a deeper safety hidden beneath surrender. Love becomes both the path and the protection.

This is why the ancient writer compares contemplation to sleep.

In ordinary sleep, the body ceases its labor for a time so it may be restored and strengthened. In the same way, contemplative prayer allows the restless inner faculties to grow quiet. The endless questions, anxieties, fantasies, and arguments slowly settle. The soul rests from its constant striving and simply remains before God in naked trust.

There is a profound healing in this kind of rest.

The soul no longer clings to identities, achievements, emotions, or spiritual self-images. It comes before God without decoration. Without performance. Without trying to manufacture holiness. And in that simplicity, something deep within begins to soften and heal.

Be careful, then, not to clothe this sacred stillness with too many thoughts about yourself. The moment the mind grasps tightly at experiences, labels, or spiritual accomplishments, distraction quickly returns. The soul becomes scattered again among countless inner movements.

But when you remain simple before God—quietly open, gently surrendered, lovingly attentive—the heart begins learning another kind of wisdom. A wisdom that does not shout. A wisdom that rests. A wisdom born not from mastery, but from communion.

### Breath Prayer

> **Inhale:** *Quiet my restless mind*

> **Exhale:** *Teach me to rest in You*
