## 46 - The Faithfulness Hidden in Dry Seasons

> *Grace remains even when its sweetness fades.*

There are seasons when prayer feels alive and God's presence seems close. Love rises naturally within you, and even ordinary moments carry a quiet sense of wonder. Yet there are also seasons when that sweetness fades. The longing that once felt vibrant grows dim, and the silence that once felt comforting seems empty. When this happens, it is easy to wonder whether something has gone wrong.

Do not lose heart when you find yourself in such a season. The spiritual journey is not a straight path but a crossing. You leave behind familiar ways of experiencing God before fully arriving at what lies ahead. In the uncertainty, old landmarks disappear and faith is asked to continue without the reassurance it once enjoyed.

One of the deepest lessons of contemplation is learning that God's presence is not measured by your feelings. When consolation fills the heart, God is present. When consolation fades, God is still present. The cloud may hide the sun, but it does not extinguish its light. In the same way, grace continues its hidden work even when you can no longer sense its warmth.

Sometimes dryness becomes a gift. As long as love depends upon spiritual comfort, it remains tied to what it receives. But when the sweetness fades and you continue turning toward God, love begins to mature. Trust deepens. The heart learns to remain faithful not because it feels rewarded, but because it has discovered that God is worthy of love in every season.

Over time, this quiet faithfulness brings a deeper freedom. You become less attached to particular experiences and more anchored in God's presence itself. Whether prayer feels full or empty, whether consolation comes or goes, your heart learns to rest in the One who never leaves. In that surrender, love grows simple, steady, and strong, discovering that God's hidden work continues even in the silence.

**Breath Prayer**

> Inhale: *You are here in the silence*
>
> Exhale: *I trust Your hidden work*
