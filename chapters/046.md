## 46 — The Faithfulness Hidden in Dry Seasons

> *Grace remains even when its sweetness fades.*

There are seasons on the contemplative path when the warmth of God's presence seems close and unmistakable. Prayer feels alive. Love rises naturally within the heart. The soul is carried by a quiet joy that softens everything it touches. In such moments, even ordinary life seems illuminated from within.

Yet there are other seasons when all of this appears to vanish.

The desire that once burned brightly feels distant. The sweetness that once accompanied prayer can no longer be found. You may sit in silence and feel as though nothing is happening at all. The practices that once brought comfort seem dry, and even familiar ways of approaching God no longer satisfy. It can feel as though you have been left somewhere between what once was and what has not yet come.

When such times arrive, do not lose heart.

The ancient writer compares this season to crossing a sea. You are no longer standing firmly on the shore you once knew, yet neither have you fully arrived at the new land toward which God is leading you. The waters feel uncertain. Winds rise unexpectedly. Familiar landmarks disappear. Yet the crossing itself is part of the journey.

In these moments, the soul learns a deeper trust.

Often we imagine that God's nearness can be measured by what we feel. When consolation fills the heart, we assume God is close. When consolation fades, we fear God has withdrawn. But the deeper wisdom of the contemplative life gently challenges this assumption. God's presence is not dependent upon our ability to perceive it. The sun does not cease to shine because clouds have hidden it from view.

What often disappears is not grace itself, but our awareness of its sweetness.

The warmth, tenderness, longing, and delight we experience in prayer are precious gifts. They encourage us, nourish us, and awaken our love for God. Yet these experiences are not the same as grace itself. Grace works far deeper than feeling. It continues its hidden labor beneath the surface of awareness, even when the heart feels empty and the mind feels barren.

For this reason, God sometimes allows the soul to pass through seasons of dryness.

Not as punishment.

Not as abandonment.

But as a gentle invitation into a more mature love.

As long as we depend entirely upon spiritual feelings, our love remains somewhat attached to the comfort those feelings bring. But when the sweetness fades and we continue to offer ourselves to God, something beautiful begins to emerge. Love becomes less dependent on reward. Trust grows stronger. The soul learns to remain present even when no consolation arrives.

This hidden work forms a deeper freedom within us.

Gradually, we discover that our peace does not rest upon spiritual experiences but upon God alone. We become willing to receive consolation when it comes and willing to release it when it goes. The heart no longer clings. It simply rests. Whether God feels near or far, the soul remains turned toward Him in quiet fidelity.

And this, perhaps, is one of the most beautiful transformations of all.

The mature contemplative no longer seeks God merely for the sweetness God provides. The soul seeks God for God's own sake. Presence is enough. Love is enough. Even silence becomes enough.

In that surrender, the soul is slowly clothed in the life of God Himself. The old need to grasp, possess, and control begins to fall away. What remains is a quiet union of love—a simple willingness to be with God exactly as He chooses to give Himself in each moment.

Whether in consolation or desolation, He is still here.

And grace is still at work.
