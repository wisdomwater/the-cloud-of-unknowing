## 64 — The Holy Longing

> *The soul finds its deepest peace not in possessing, but in continually turning toward Love.*

The longing for God is itself a gift. Whenever the heart reaches beyond itself, seeking something deeper than comfort, success, or certainty, it is already responding to a quiet invitation from grace. The ancient writers called this movement aspiration—a holy yearning that lifts the soul toward God. Like a traveler walking toward home, the soul is drawn by something it has not yet fully reached but somehow already knows.

There are moments when this longing gives way to a deeper rest. The heart becomes still, and for a time it seems to dwell in a simple awareness of God's presence. In those moments, the seeking falls silent because the soul has found what it was seeking. Love rests in Love. Yet in this life such moments rarely remain forever. The soul eventually returns to ordinary awareness, and the longing awakens once more.

This rhythm is not a failure of prayer. It is part of its beauty. The soul rises toward God, rests for a time, then rises again. Each movement of longing deepens desire. Each return to seeking enlarges the heart's capacity for love. What feels like repetition is often transformation occurring beneath the surface.

The author speaks strongly about the unhappiness that comes when the soul settles for less than this deeper calling. Human beings were made for communion with God. When we seek fulfillment only in external things, something within remains restless. Even good things cannot satisfy the deepest hunger of the heart. There is a thirst within us that points beyond itself.

Yet this longing should never be understood as striving driven by anxiety or spiritual ambition. The aspiration of which the mystics speak is not the ambition of the ego trying to become important. It is the quiet attraction of love. It is the heart being gently drawn toward the One for whom it was created. The more this longing is purified, the less it becomes about gaining something and the more it becomes about surrendering ourselves into the mystery of God.

The spiritual journey contains countless variations. No two souls are led in exactly the same way. Some experience prayer as quiet trust. Others encounter seasons of intense longing. Some move gradually through many stages, while others seem to be carried unexpectedly into deeper waters. The important thing is not to compare paths but to remain attentive to the invitations being offered in the present moment.

As the soul matures, prayer becomes simpler. Thoughts, images, and concepts begin to loosen their grip. The heart discovers that it does not need to grasp every idea about God in order to love Him. It learns to release distractions, not through force, but through gentle returning. Again and again, attention is brought back to the One who waits beneath every thought.

The ancient writer calls this practice placing all lesser things beneath the "cloud of forgetting." It is not a rejection of the world, nor a denial of its goodness. Rather, it is the recognition that for a little while, the soul desires to rest in something deeper. Thoughts may come and go. Memories may arise. Concerns may present themselves. Yet the heart quietly returns to God, choosing love over distraction.

This path unfolds gradually. A person may begin with repentance, reflection, and deliberate prayer. Over time, those practices may open into deeper trust, surrender, and love. There is no need to rush. God leads each soul according to its capacity. The only task is to remain faithful to the light that has been given today.

The holy longing itself is already evidence that God is near.
