## 13 — Love Makes the Heart Spacious

> *The deeper the soul rests in God,*
>
> *the less tightly it clings*
>
> *to itself alone.*

As contemplative prayer deepens, something subtle begins changing in the way you hold other people.

At first, spiritual longing can feel very personal. You become aware of your own inner life, your own wounds, your own hunger for peace, healing, or God. Much of the early journey involves simply learning how to become quiet enough to notice what is happening within you.

But over time, genuine contemplation softens the boundaries of the heart.

You begin seeing others differently.

Not as categories.

Not as opponents.

Not merely as useful or difficult people moving through your life.

But as souls carrying hidden burdens, fears, longings, and wounds much like your own.

And strangely, this tenderness often grows most deeply in silence.

The contemplative path is not cold withdrawal from humanity. True prayer does not harden the heart into spiritual isolation. If anything, real communion with God slowly dissolves the illusion that you exist separately from everyone else.

For when the soul rests quietly before Love itself, it becomes harder to hold hatred tightly.

Harder to nourish resentment.

Harder to divide the world so sharply into “us” and “them.”

The ancient writer speaks boldly here, saying that the contemplative eventually begins holding friend and stranger, loved one and enemy, with the same deep goodwill before God. Not because human relationships become identical, and not because affection disappears, but because the soul begins loving from a deeper place than preference alone.

You may still feel naturally close to certain people.

You may still grieve, struggle, set boundaries, or experience conflict.

Love does not erase human complexity.

But beneath all of it, something quieter begins emerging:

a sincere desire for the good of all.

Even for those who misunderstand you.

Even for those who wound you.

This does not happen through force.

You cannot shame yourself into universal compassion.

The heart becomes spacious slowly.

Silently.

Like a lake becoming still after wind has passed over it for many hours.

And this spaciousness grows because contemplative prayer keeps returning the soul to its true center. Beneath all the restless identities we cling to, beneath the endless judgments and defenses of the mind, there is a deeper place where every human being stands equally dependent upon mercy.

Equally held within mystery.

Equally loved by God.

The contemplative life slowly teaches you to live from this deeper place.

And yet this path also contains effort.

At times, great effort.

For one of the hardest parts of contemplation is not loving God.

The deeper longing for God is often already there within you.

The difficulty lies in releasing the endless noise competing for your attention.

Thoughts pull constantly.

Memories.

Plans.

Anxieties.

Imagined conversations.

Regrets.

The mind runs in circles trying to secure itself through control, understanding, and repetition.

And so the work of contemplation often feels very simple, yet very difficult:

gently returning.

Again and again.

Not violently suppressing thoughts.

Not attacking yourself for distraction.

But quietly placing all things beneath the cloud of forgetting for a little while so the heart may rest more fully in God.

This takes patience.

And at first, it can feel exhausting.

You may sit in silence only to discover how restless your inner world truly is. You may become discouraged by how quickly the mind wanders or how difficult stillness feels.

But do not lose heart.

The early labor of prayer is not failure.

It is preparation.

You are slowly learning how to loosen your grip on constant mental activity so that a deeper awareness may awaken beneath it.

And over time, something beautiful begins happening.

What once felt strained becomes gentler.

What once required effort begins unfolding more naturally.

Moments of stillness appear unexpectedly.

The heart settles more quickly.

Love rises more quietly on its own.

And you begin realizing that the deepest movement of contemplation was never something you manufactured by your own strength.

God was already waiting beneath the noise.

Patiently.

Tenderly.

Drawing you inward all along.

## Breath Prayer

> Breath in: *Quiet my restless heart*
>
> Breath out: *Teach me deeper love*
