## 13 — Love Makes the Heart Spacious

> *The deeper the soul rests in God, the less tightly it clings to itself alone.*

As contemplative prayer deepens, something subtle begins to change in the way you hold other people within your heart. In the beginning, spiritual longing often feels intensely personal. You become aware of your own wounds, your own fears, your own longing for peace, healing, and God. Much of the early journey is simply learning how to become quiet enough to notice what is happening within you at all.

But over time, genuine contemplation begins softening the boundaries of the heart.

You slowly begin seeing others differently. Not merely as categories, opinions, obstacles, or companions passing through your life, but as souls carrying hidden burdens much like your own. Beneath outward behavior, beneath conflict and misunderstanding, you begin sensing the shared ache that runs quietly through human life. And strangely, this tenderness often grows most deeply in silence.

The contemplative path is not a cold withdrawal from humanity. True prayer does not harden the soul into spiritual isolation or superiority. If anything, real communion with God slowly dissolves the illusion that you exist separately from everyone else. For when the heart rests quietly before Love itself, it becomes harder to cling tightly to hatred. Harder to nourish resentment. Harder to divide the world so sharply into “us” and “them.”

The ancient contemplatives spoke boldly about this transformation. They taught that the soul slowly learns to hold friend and stranger, loved one and enemy, within the same deep goodwill before God. This does not mean all relationships become identical, nor does it erase the reality of grief, conflict, or necessary boundaries. Human life remains complex, and love does not remove that complexity.

But beneath it all, something quieter begins emerging: a sincere desire for the good of all people.

Even for those who misunderstand you.

Even for those who wound you.

This kind of spaciousness cannot be forced into existence. You cannot shame yourself into compassion, nor manufacture mercy through sheer effort. The heart becomes spacious slowly, almost invisibly, like a lake growing still after the wind has finally passed over it.

And this spaciousness grows because contemplative prayer continually returns the soul to its true center. Beneath the restless identities we cling to, beneath the endless judgments, fears, and defenses of the mind, there is a deeper place where every human being stands equally dependent upon mercy. Equally fragile. Equally held within mystery. Equally loved by God.

The contemplative life slowly teaches you to live from this deeper place.

And yet this path also contains effort. At times, very great effort.

For one of the hardest parts of contemplation is not loving God. Often the longing for God is already quietly alive within you. The greater difficulty lies in releasing the endless noise competing for your attention. Thoughts pull constantly at the mind. Memories return uninvited. Plans, anxieties, imagined conversations, regrets, and fears circle endlessly, trying to secure some sense of control or certainty.

And so the work of contemplation often appears very simple, while feeling very difficult.

You gently return.

Again and again, you return.

Not by violently suppressing thoughts. Not by attacking yourself for distraction or restlessness. But by quietly placing all things beneath the cloud of forgetting for a little while, allowing the heart to rest more fully in God.

This takes patience. And in the beginning, it can feel exhausting.

You may sit in silence only to discover how restless your inner world truly is. You may become discouraged by how quickly the mind wanders or how difficult stillness feels to sustain. But do not lose heart. The early labor of prayer is not failure. It is preparation.

You are slowly learning how to loosen your grip on constant mental activity so that a deeper awareness may awaken beneath it.

And over time, something beautiful begins happening. What once felt strained begins becoming gentler. What once required tremendous effort slowly unfolds more naturally. Moments of stillness arise unexpectedly. The heart settles more quickly. Love begins rising quietly on its own, without being forced.

And eventually you begin realizing that the deepest movement of contemplation was never something you manufactured through your own strength. God was already waiting beneath the noise all along.

Patiently.

Tenderly.

Drawing you inward through love from the very beginning.

### Breath Prayer

> **Inhale:** *Quiet my restless heart*
>
> **Exhale:** *Teach me deeper love*
