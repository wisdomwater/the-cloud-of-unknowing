## 28 — God Is Not Somewhere Far Away

> *Love discovers the Presence already near.*

One of the quiet misunderstandings that often appears along the contemplative path is the feeling that God exists somewhere far away from us. Many people carry this assumption without even realizing it. Heaven feels distant. God feels removed from ordinary life, hidden somewhere “above” us spiritually, emotionally, or cosmically. And because of this, prayer can slowly become filled with strain — a reaching toward something that always feels just beyond our grasp.

But the ancient contemplatives gently challenge this illusion. God is not found through mental striving or spiritual performance. The soul does not discover divine nearness by climbing upward through imagination or forcing itself into dramatic mystical experiences. True contemplation is not an escape into fantasy. It is an awakening into reality. And reality, at its deepest level, is already filled with the presence of God.

The ancient writer observed how easily the mind becomes fascinated with visions, inward imagery, mystical sensations, or imagined revelations. Some people begin straining mentally upward, trying to force transcendence through spiritual imagination. Yet contemplation moves in another direction entirely. It invites the soul away from grasping and into quiet openness.

Over time, prayer often becomes simpler rather than more elaborate. The soul slowly stops trying to manufacture spiritual experiences and instead learns how to rest gently within awareness itself. Not an image of God. Not a performance for God. But a quiet inward surrender to Presence.

This matters because the imagination is powerful. The human mind naturally creates images, stories, symbols, expectations, and inward worlds. And imagination itself is not the enemy. Beauty, poetry, sacred symbols, and holy stories can nourish the soul deeply. They can awaken longing, tenderness, reverence, and wonder.

But contemplation gently teaches us not to confuse the symbol with the Reality it points toward.

For God is always greater than the images we create. Greater than our concepts. Greater than our inward pictures and spiritual language. No thought can contain Him completely. No image can fully hold the mystery of divine Love.

The ancient contemplatives point toward something profoundly freeing here: sacred things are invitations, not destinations. Stories, rituals, symbols, visions, and outward forms carry meaning, but they are not ends in themselves. They are more like windows opening toward deeper reality. Like a cup carrying living water. Like a tree bearing fruit.

The problem is not in loving the symbol. The problem comes when the soul clings to the symbol while missing the living Presence shining through it.

And so contemplative prayer slowly loosens the mind’s need for certainty through images and explanations. The soul learns instead how to remain present within mystery. Not cold abstraction. Not emptiness. But living mystery — the mystery of a Love too vast to be captured by imagination alone.

And strangely, when the soul stops straining so hard to “reach upward,” it often begins discovering how near God has always been.

Closer than thought. Closer than image. Closer even than emotion. Quietly present beneath every breath and within every ordinary moment of life.

The contemplative path does not ask you to reject beauty, sacred stories, or meaningful symbols. These things can guide and nourish the heart tenderly. But it gently reminds you not to mistake the finger pointing toward the moon for the moon itself.

The symbol is not the Presence. The image is not the Reality. The idea is not Love itself.

And so prayer gradually becomes less about climbing spiritually upward and more about softening inwardly into what already surrounds and sustains you. A quiet awareness begins emerging beneath the noise of striving. A stillness slowly opens within the heart.

And there, often in great simplicity, the soul discovers that God was never truly absent at all. Only hidden beneath the noise. And when the noise grows quiet, even for a moment, Love begins shining through ordinary life with surprising nearness.

### Breath Prayer

> **Inhale:** *You are nearer than I imagine*
>
> **Exhale:** *Teach me to rest in Presence*
