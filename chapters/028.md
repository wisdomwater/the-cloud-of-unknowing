## 28 — God Is Not Somewhere Far Away

> *The soul does not reach God*
>
> *by climbing upward.*
>
> *Love discovers what is already near.*

One of the subtle misunderstandings on the contemplative path is the idea that God exists somewhere far away.

Many people carry this feeling unconsciously. They imagine heaven as distant. They imagine God as removed from ordinary life, hidden somewhere “above” them spiritually, emotionally, or cosmically. And because of this, prayer can begin to feel like a desperate reaching toward something endlessly out of reach.

The ancient contemplatives gently challenge this illusion.

God is not found through strained imagination.

Not through mentally climbing into the heavens.

Not through forcing the mind into dramatic spiritual imagery.

The soul does not ascend to God through fantasy.

It awakens through love.

The ancient writer describes how some people become fascinated with inward visions, spiritual imagery, heavenly pictures, mystical sounds, and imagined revelations. They strain mentally upward, trying to force transcendence through the imagination.

But contemplation moves differently than this.

True prayer is not an escape into fantasy.

It is a deepening into reality.

And reality, at its deepest level, is already saturated with the presence of God.

This is why contemplative prayer often becomes simpler over time rather than more elaborate. The soul slowly stops trying to manufacture spiritual experiences and instead begins resting more quietly in direct awareness of Love itself.

Not an image of God.

Not a performance for God.

But a gentle inward openness to Presence.

This matters because the imagination is powerful.

The human mind naturally creates images, stories, expectations, and symbolic worlds. And while imagination itself is not bad — indeed, beauty and symbolism can nourish the soul deeply — contemplation invites us beyond clinging to mental images as though they were God Himself.

For God is always greater than the images we create.

Greater than our concepts.

Greater than our inward pictures.

Greater even than our spiritual language.

The ancient writer points toward something profoundly freeing here: spiritual truth is often hidden beneath the surface appearance of things.

Sacred stories, visions, symbols, rituals, and outward gestures all carry meaning, but they are not ends in themselves. They are invitations pointing beyond themselves toward deeper reality.

Like a cup holding living water.

Like a tree bearing fruit.

The mistake is not in appreciating the symbol.

The mistake is in clinging to the symbol while missing the living reality it points toward.

This is why contemplative prayer gradually loosens the mind’s need to grasp God through visual certainty. The soul learns instead how to remain present within mystery.

Not empty mystery.

Not cold abstraction.

But living mystery.

The mystery of a Love too vast to be captured by imagination alone.

And strangely, when the soul stops straining so hard to “reach upward,” it often begins discovering how near God has always been.

Closer than thought.

Closer than image.

Closer than emotion.

Quietly present beneath every breath.

The contemplative path does not ask you to abandon beauty, sacred stories, or meaningful symbols. These things can nourish and guide the heart tenderly. But it gently reminds you not to confuse the finger pointing toward the moon with the moon itself.

The symbol is not the Presence.

The image is not the Reality.

The idea is not the Love itself.

And so prayer slowly becomes less about climbing spiritually upward and more about softening inwardly into what already surrounds and sustains you.

A quiet awareness.

A surrendered openness.

A stillness beneath striving.

The soul discovers that God was never truly absent.

Only hidden beneath the noise.

And when the noise quiets, even slightly, Love begins shining through ordinary life with surprising nearness.

## Breath Prayer

> Breath in: *You are nearer than I imagine*
>
> Breath out: *Teach me to rest in Presence*
