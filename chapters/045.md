## 45 — The Work That God Does Within

> *The deepest prayer is not something we accomplish. It is something we allow.*

There are many good and necessary works in the spiritual life. We learn, serve, pray, love, forgive, and grow through countless small acts of faithfulness. In these outward and active expressions of devotion, our minds, wills, and efforts all have an important place. We participate consciously, making choices that help shape the direction of our lives.

But contemplative prayer unfolds differently.

As we move toward deeper silence and communion with God, there comes a point where wisdom alone cannot carry us further. The intellect, valuable as it is, reaches the edge of its own usefulness. Here, God becomes the primary worker, and the soul's role changes from striving to consenting. We do not manufacture divine union through effort. We simply become willing to receive what God longs to give.

This is one way of understanding Christ's words: "Without me, you can do nothing."

In every part of life, God is already present. Every breath, every movement, every moment of existence rests within the sustaining love of God. Yet the manner of that presence differs. Sometimes God allows us the freedom to wander. Sometimes God strengthens and guides our faithful actions. But in contemplative prayer, God works most directly, drawing the soul inward through grace while asking only for our willingness and surrender.

The spiritual journey often reveals three broad conditions of the human heart. There are times when we live disconnected from God, times when we actively cooperate with grace through prayer and service, and times when we are drawn into deeper contemplation where God becomes the principal actor within us. In every stage, God remains near. Yet as the soul matures, it learns that some of the most profound transformations happen not through effort, but through consent.

This understanding protects us from a common mistake. We often imagine that spiritual growth depends entirely on what we can accomplish. We become preoccupied with methods, techniques, disciplines, and achievements. Yet the deeper mysteries of God cannot be seized. They can only be received.

The contemplative path is not about becoming spiritually impressive. It is about becoming increasingly available.

Little by little, we learn to release our need to control the process. We learn to trust the hidden work unfolding beneath our awareness. And in that surrender, the soul begins to discover a love that carries it further than effort ever could.

> **Inhale:** *You are the One who works*
