## 53 — Revealing What Has Always Been Hidden

> *The soul does not create the light. It removes what obscures it.*

The contemplative life often feels like a search for something missing.

We imagine that God is distant, hidden somewhere beyond our reach. We assume that if we could only learn enough, understand enough, pray enough, or become holy enough, we might finally discover what we seek.

Yet the ancient contemplatives saw the journey differently.

They believed that God is not absent. Rather, our awareness is veiled. The deepest work of prayer is not adding something new to the soul, but removing what prevents us from seeing what has always been present.

The ancient writer returns to the image of a sculptor.

Imagine a beautiful figure concealed within a block of stone. The sculptor does not invent the image. He simply removes everything that does not belong. Piece by piece, the excess falls away until the hidden beauty stands revealed.

So it is with the spiritual life.

The divine presence does not need to be created within us. God's life already surrounds us, sustains us, and dwells at the deepest center of our being. Yet our fears, attachments, distractions, assumptions, and endless mental noise can obscure our awareness of that reality.

The contemplative path is therefore a path of gentle subtraction.

We release what is unnecessary.

We loosen our grip on what is false.

We surrender our need to possess God through concepts, experiences, or certainty.

And in that surrender, something hidden begins to emerge.

The ancient writer speaks of a beauty concealed beneath countless layers. In modern language, we might say that beneath all our roles, identities, anxieties, and stories there remains a deeper self rooted in God. The work of prayer is not becoming someone else. It is allowing that deeper reality to become visible.

This process requires grace.

We cannot force it.

We cannot manufacture it through willpower alone.

Like a sculptor patiently revealing what already exists within the stone, grace slowly removes what obscures our vision. The work is often quiet and gradual. Much of it happens beneath the surface where we cannot easily measure or understand it.

This is why the contemplative tradition speaks so often of letting go.

When we speak about God, we begin with the most beautiful words we know. We call God good, loving, wise, faithful, merciful, and holy. These affirmations are true. They guide the heart toward what is real.

But eventually, even these sacred words must be held lightly.

For God is more than our idea of goodness.

More than our understanding of wisdom.

More than our experience of love.

Every affirmation is true, yet none are complete.

And so the soul learns another movement: the movement of release.

After speaking, it becomes silent.

After grasping, it opens its hands.

After understanding, it rests in wonder.

The ancient writer describes this as moving from affirmation into holy unknowing. We gradually let go of every image and concept, not because they are wrong, but because God surpasses them all. In doing so, we begin to glimpse the mystery hidden behind every sacred word.

What remains is not emptiness.

What remains is Presence.

A presence so simple that the mind often overlooks it.

A presence so constant that we mistake it for ordinary awareness.

A presence hidden beneath every thought, every prayer, every longing, and every breath.

The contemplative path does not lead us away from reality.

It leads us more deeply into it.

And there, beneath all the layers we have accumulated, we discover that the light we sought was never absent. It was waiting quietly beneath everything we thought we were.

### Breath Prayer

> **Inhale:** *Remove what obscures*
>
> **Exhale:** *Reveal Your hidden light*
