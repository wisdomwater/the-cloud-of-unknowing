## 76 — Returning Again to God

> *The wandering mind is not defeated by force, but by returning to love.*

One of the great discoveries of the spiritual life is that distractions never entirely disappear. No matter how sincerely we seek God, thoughts continue to arise. Memories surface. Worries intrude. Old habits reappear. The mind drifts toward unfinished conversations, future plans, past failures, and countless concerns.

The author reminds us that even the most mature souls are not completely free from such experiences. As long as we live in this world, thoughts about created things will continue to present themselves. This is simply part of the human condition. The goal of contemplation is not to achieve a perfectly empty mind, but to learn how to return again and again to God.

Many people become discouraged by distractions. They assume that wandering thoughts mean they are failing at prayer. Yet the contemplative tradition offers a gentler perspective. The appearance of a distraction is not the problem. The deeper question is what we do when we notice it.

Do we become frustrated?

Do we begin fighting with our thoughts?

Do we abandon prayer altogether?

Or do we quietly return?

The author repeatedly recommends the same simple remedy: transcend the distraction and continue turning toward God. Not by force. Not by argument. Not by becoming fascinated with the distraction itself. Simply by looking beyond it.

He offers a beautiful image. We are to look over the shoulders of our distracting thoughts, as though we are waiting for Someone standing behind them.

That Someone is God.

The thought may still be present.

The memory may still linger.

The worry may still echo in the background.

Yet the soul gently shifts its attention beyond them.

This movement of returning is itself an act of love.

The author is especially careful to note that even thoughts about our sins can become distractions. There is a time for repentance, examination, and honest self-reflection. Yet once the soul has sincerely confessed, surrendered, and turned toward God, endless preoccupation with past failures can itself become an obstacle.

The contemplative path is not about staring forever at our brokenness.

It is about moving through our brokenness toward God.

Love heals what self-accusation cannot.

The author then describes the heart's longing for God as a strong and persistent desire. Yet he is not speaking about emotional intensity. He does not mean a dramatic spiritual excitement or a restless craving. Rather, he points toward a deeper determination within the soul.

A quiet resolve.

A steady willingness.

A refusal to give up.

This deeper longing remains even when emotions fade. It remains when prayer feels dry. It remains when distractions multiply. It remains because its source is not human enthusiasm but grace.

The author also reflects on the roots of temptation. Some distractions arise from old habits, wounds, and patterns we have carried throughout life. Others arise simply from our shared human condition. Either way, we need not be shocked by them.

The presence of temptation is not failure.

The presence of struggle is not defeat.

What matters is where the heart ultimately turns.

Again and again, the contemplative life comes back to this simple truth: God is the one who draws the soul. We cooperate, but we do not manufacture the journey. We respond, but we do not create the longing. Even our desire for God is itself a gift.

This realization brings great freedom.

The burden does not rest entirely upon our shoulders.

We are not climbing toward God alone.

God is already reaching toward us.

Indeed, the author suggests that the deepest movements of prayer arise not from our own cleverness or effort but from God's quiet activity within the heart. Sometimes this movement expresses itself through silence. Sometimes through a brief inward turning. Sometimes through a simple spoken prayer.

A single word may be enough.

A whispered "Lord."

A quiet "Love."

A simple "Yes."

When such words arise from the heart, they become more than speech. They become the soul's response to the One who has been calling all along.

And so when distractions come—as they surely will—the invitation remains unchanged.

Do not become discouraged.

Do not become fascinated by them.

Simply look beyond them.

And return again to love.

### Breath Prayer

> **Inhale:** *Beyond every distraction*
>
> **Exhale:** *I return to You*
