## 10 — The Quiet Work Hidden From Others

> *Not every sacred thing can be explained to those who have not yet felt it.*

There are seasons in the spiritual life when the soul begins withdrawing from noise. Not from love, compassion, or responsibility, but from unnecessary striving. The heart becomes less interested in proving itself, defending itself, or remaining endlessly entangled in outward activity. Beneath the surface, a deeper longing begins awakening—a longing for stillness, prayer, presence, and communion with God beyond words.

And often, other people do not understand this at all.

They may think you are becoming distant, impractical, unproductive, or too inward. Some may even question your sincerity or quietly assume you are avoiding life itself. This has always happened to contemplative souls, because the hidden life of prayer rarely appears impressive from the outside. It does not always produce visible accomplishments, and much of its work unfolds silently beneath the surface where no one else can fully see it.

Because of this hiddenness, people often misunderstand it. Even good people. Even loving people. Even people sincerely devoted to God.

The ancient story of Martha and Mary speaks gently to this tension. Martha remained busy with necessary work—serving, caring, preparing. Her labor mattered, and it was not condemned. Love often expresses itself through action, responsibility, and service to others. But Mary sat quietly in attentive presence. She listened. She rested. She received.

And Martha could not fully understand what was happening within her sister.

From the outside, Mary’s stillness may have appeared passive or unhelpful, perhaps even irresponsible. But inwardly, she was being drawn into something deeper than outward productivity alone. The contemplative life often looks this way. Much of its transformation happens invisibly while the soul sits quietly before God and something subtle unfolds beneath ordinary awareness.

The restless need to perform begins loosening. The inner noise softens. The heart slowly becomes more spacious, receptive, and grounded in love itself.

And because this work is hidden, contemplatives are often tempted to defend themselves—to explain, justify, or convince others that their inward life matters. But contemplation slowly teaches another way. You do not need to constantly argue for the validity of what God is doing within you. Not every sacred movement can be translated into language. Some things can only be lived.

Often the wisest response to misunderstanding is not defensiveness, but gentleness.

For many who question the contemplative path are not malicious. They simply do not yet understand the hunger that draws the soul inward. Someone who has never tasted silence deeply may struggle to understand why another person longs for it so profoundly. And so the contemplative learns compassion toward those who cannot yet see what they themselves are only beginning to trust.

This includes compassion toward yourself.

Because there may be seasons where even you begin questioning the hiddenness of this path. You may wonder whether your quiet prayer matters, whether your simple returning matters, whether sitting silently before God changes anything at all. But the deepest spiritual work is often the least visible. A seed grows in darkness long before fruit appears. Water moves silently beneath the roots of trees. Dawn arrives gradually while most of the world is still asleep.

So too, contemplation reshapes the soul quietly.

Not through dramatic displays, but through hidden faithfulness.

You return to prayer. You release distraction again. You offer your longing to God even when you cannot feel anything clearly. And little by little, love deepens beneath the surface. You become less reactive, less hurried, less ruled by fear or constant self-protection—not because you forced yourself into holiness, but because you remained available to Presence.

And this quiet availability slowly changes everything.

So if others misunderstand your longing for stillness, do not harden your heart against them. Bless them gently. Continue loving them well. And continue returning inward toward the quiet place where God waits for you beyond all explanation.
