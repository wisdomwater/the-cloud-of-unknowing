## 8 — The Humility That Love Creates

> *The soul softens most deeply not through shame, but through love.*

Many people imagine spiritual growth as a matter of becoming stricter with themselves—trying harder, controlling more, punishing weakness, and forcing transformation through sheer effort. But the contemplative path moves differently. It does not begin with harshness toward the self. It begins with a quiet turning toward God in love. And strangely, this gentle turning begins transforming the soul more deeply than force ever could.

There are many outward practices that can help awaken devotion. Silence, fasting, simplicity, prayer, self-examination, acts of mercy, repentance—these can all prepare the heart. They soften the inner soil and loosen the grip of distraction and self-centeredness. Yet none of these practices, by themselves, can heal the deepest root of separation within us. Only love reaches there. Only the quiet surrender of the heart to God begins untangling the deeper knots hidden beneath the surface.

This is why contemplation is not merely another spiritual discipline among many. It is the slow consenting of the soul to divine love itself. And where love is allowed to deepen honestly, transformation begins unfolding naturally—not perfectly, not instantly, but truly. Over time, the heart becomes gentler and more spacious. It becomes less driven by the need to defend itself constantly, less consumed by comparison, performance, or self-importance.

This is the beginning of humility.

Not humiliation. Not self-hatred. Not pretending you are worthless.

True humility is simply learning to see yourself clearly without turning away. You begin recognizing both your fragility and your belovedness at the same time. You see how restless the mind can be, how easily fear shapes your reactions, how often the ego longs for approval, comfort, control, or certainty. And yet beneath all of this, you also begin sensing something even deeper: that God remains near to you still.

Not because you have become spiritually impressive.

But because Love itself is merciful.

This realization softens the soul in ways self-condemnation never can. Many people secretly believe humility means thinking poorly of themselves, but contemplation reveals something far gentler. The more deeply you encounter God, the less obsessed you become with yourself altogether. You no longer feel the same need to constantly prove your worth or construct an identity around appearing wise, spiritual, successful, or superior.

The heart slowly relaxes.

Not because you have mastered yourself, but because you are beginning to realize you are being held.

And there are moments in deep prayer where this becomes almost wordless. For a brief instant, you may forget your spiritual self-image entirely. You are no longer measuring your holiness, replaying your failures, or trying to become extraordinary. You simply rest inside Presence. And in those quiet moments, humility becomes complete without effort, because the soul is no longer staring endlessly at itself. It is turned toward Love.

This does not mean you ignore your wounds or pretend your struggles are unimportant. Honest self-awareness remains necessary. The contemplative life requires sincerity, because you cannot surrender what you refuse to acknowledge. But there is a difference between honest awareness and endless self-fixation. One opens the heart. The other exhausts it.

So do not become discouraged when you notice your weakness. Do not become frightened when you see how much fear, distraction, or longing still lives within you. This seeing is not failure. It is awakening. The soul is becoming more truthful, and truth, when held gently inside love, becomes healing.

Little by little, contemplation teaches you that holiness is not found in becoming spiritually impressive. It is found in becoming available—available to grace, available to tenderness, available to the quiet work of God unfolding beneath your striving.

And perhaps this is why contemplative prayer so often feels hidden and small. You sit quietly before God. You return when distracted. You offer your simple love again. And beneath this ordinary faithfulness, the soul is slowly being remade.

### Breath Prayer

> **Inhale:** *Teach me gentle humility*
>
> **Exhale:** *I rest within Your love*
