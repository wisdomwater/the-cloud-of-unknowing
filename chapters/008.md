## 8 - The Humility That Love Creates

> *The soul softens most deeply not through shame, but through love.*

Many people approach spiritual growth through effort, striving, and self-correction. Yet the contemplative path begins somewhere gentler. It begins with turning toward God in love and allowing that love to do its quiet work within you. What force cannot accomplish, love often transforms from the inside out.

Practices such as prayer, silence, repentance, and simplicity can prepare the heart for this transformation. They help loosen the grip of distraction and create space for deeper awareness. But lasting change comes not from mastering spiritual disciplines, but from consenting to the love that meets you within them.

As love deepens, humility begins to emerge naturally. This is not humiliation or self-rejection. It is the ability to see yourself honestly—to recognize both your limitations and your belovedness at the same time. You become aware of your fears, distractions, and longing for control, yet you also discover that God's presence remains with you in the midst of them.

This realization softens the need to prove yourself. You become less concerned with appearing wise, spiritual, or successful, and more interested in simply being present. In moments of quiet prayer, you may even forget yourself for a while, resting in a love that asks nothing from you except your willingness to remain.

When you notice weakness within yourself, do not be discouraged. Honest seeing is not failure; it is part of awakening. Little by little, contemplation teaches you that holiness is not found in becoming impressive, but in becoming available—available to grace, available to tenderness, and available to the transforming love already at work within you.

**Breath Prayer**

> Inhale: *Teach me gentle humility*
>
> Exhale: *I rest within Your love*
