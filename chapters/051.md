## 51 — Beyond Every Word and Image

> *God is not found at the end of our thoughts, but beyond them.*

The soul's journey toward God begins with many words.

We learn through teachings, scriptures, symbols, prayers, and sacred ideas. We speak of God's goodness, wisdom, beauty, mercy, and love. These are true and necessary ways of approaching the Divine. They help orient the heart and awaken desire for what is holy.

Yet every word we speak about God eventually reaches its limit.

For God is not merely the greatest thing we can imagine. God is beyond all things we can imagine. Every description points toward Him, but none can fully contain Him. Every image reveals something true, yet no image captures the whole. The soul must eventually discover that God exceeds not only our understanding but even our finest spiritual language.

This is why the ancient contemplatives taught both affirmation and surrender.

We affirm that God is good, yet God is more than what we mean by goodness. We affirm that God is wise, yet God's wisdom surpasses every human conception of wisdom. We affirm that God is loving, yet divine love reaches beyond every form of love we have known.

And so, after speaking, the soul must learn to become silent.

Not because our words are false.

But because they are incomplete.

The contemplative path is not a rejection of theology. It is its fulfillment. Words bring us to the edge of mystery. Silence allows us to enter it.

The ancient writer recalls a beautiful insight attributed to the apostle Bartholomew: that divine truth is both vast and simple, abundant and brief. God can inspire libraries of reflection, yet the deepest encounter with Him may arrive without a single word. The mystery is inexhaustible, yet somehow intimately near.

This is the paradox of the spiritual life.

The more deeply we encounter God, the more we realize that God cannot be possessed by the intellect. The soul begins to understand that divine reality is not hidden because it is distant, but because it is infinitely greater than the categories through which we normally perceive the world.

And so the seeker continues onward.

Past ideas.

Past images.

Past certainties.

Even past spiritual experiences.

Not abandoning them with contempt, but leaving them behind as one leaves a well-loved path in order to enter the forest beyond.

The Scriptures often speak of God dwelling within cloud and darkness. This darkness is not separation. It is not absence. It is the mystery that surrounds a Presence too radiant to be fully seen. What appears dark to the mind is often brilliant to the soul.

The ancient story of Moses offers a picture of this journey.

Before ascending the mountain, Moses undergoes preparation and purification. He encounters signs, wonders, voices, and radiant manifestations. He witnesses light, power, and revelation. Yet even these are not the final destination.

Beyond the voices lies silence.

Beyond the light lies mystery.

Beyond the revelation lies God Himself.

The deeper journey begins when the soul becomes willing to move beyond what can be seen and understood. There, in the hidden darkness, the heart discovers a form of knowing that does not depend upon concepts. Love continues where thought can go no further.

This is why contemplative prayer feels so simple and yet so difficult.

The mind constantly seeks something to grasp. It wants explanations, experiences, and conclusions. Yet God gently invites us beyond all grasping. The invitation is not to know less, but to trust more. Not to abandon wisdom, but to surrender the illusion that wisdom alone can contain the Infinite.

And there, beyond every word and image, the soul begins to rest.

Not because every question has been answered.

But because it has found itself held within the mystery of Love.

### Breath Prayer

> **Inhale:** *Beyond every word*
>
> **Exhale:** *I rest in Your mystery*
