## 48 — Restoring the Divine Image

> *You do not become divine by striving. You awaken to the image already planted within you.*

One of the deepest teachings of the Christian contemplative tradition is that the soul carries within itself the imprint of God.

When we become consumed by worldly desires, fears, ambitions, or attachments, we can begin to forget this. The things we love gradually shape us. What we continually give our attention to leaves its mark upon our hearts. If we become absorbed entirely in possessions, status, approval, or self-concern, our inner life slowly takes on the shape of those attachments.

Yet beneath all of these changing influences, something deeper remains.

The ancient writer reminds us that every soul is created in the image of God. This image cannot be erased. It is woven into our very being. No failure, wound, confusion, or season of wandering can completely destroy it. The image remains because it is part of what we are.

What becomes obscured is not the image itself, but the likeness.

Imagine an ancient icon darkened by years of dust and neglect. The image is still present beneath the surface, but its beauty is hidden from view. The work of the spiritual life is not creating something entirely new. It is allowing what has always been there to become visible once again.

The contemplative path is a gradual restoration.

The ancient writer speaks of the soul as possessing three capacities: mind, reason, and will. These are reflections, however faint, of the divine life itself. As grace works within us, each of these capacities begins to awaken. The mind learns trust. The reason learns hope. The will learns love.

And love is the goal toward which everything moves.

Faith opens the heart to God's reality. Hope teaches the soul to lean toward God's promises even when the path remains unclear. Love draws the whole person into union with the One who is both infinitely beyond us and intimately within us. These are not merely virtues to acquire. They are signs that the divine image is becoming increasingly visible through the way we live.

This transformation unfolds slowly.

We are often tempted to measure spiritual growth by dramatic experiences, profound insights, or extraordinary moments of prayer. Yet God frequently works through quieter means. A little more patience. A little more humility. A little more compassion toward ourselves and others. These small movements of grace gradually reshape the soul from within.

The contemplative life is not an escape from our humanity.

It is the healing of our humanity.

As the soul becomes more aligned with God, we do not become less ourselves. We become more fully who we were created to be. The false identities built from fear and striving begin to loosen their grip. The deeper self, rooted in God, begins to emerge.

And this unfolding never ends.

Every act of trust deepens the image. Every movement of hope strengthens the likeness. Every surrender into love draws the soul more deeply into the life of God. What begins as a faint awareness grows into a living participation in divine love itself.

The journey is not about becoming something other than human.

It is about becoming fully alive in the presence of God.

### Breath Prayer

> **Inhale:** *Restore Your image within me*
>
> **Exhale:** *Shape me in Your love*
