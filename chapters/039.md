## 39 — The Holy Simplicity of Being

> *God is found most deeply when the soul rests beyond its need to explain Him.*

There is a quiet freedom that comes when the soul no longer feels responsible for understanding everything about God. Much of the spiritual life begins with words, ideas, teachings, and images that help awaken love within us. These things are good and necessary. We speak of God as loving, merciful, wise, beautiful, holy, powerful, compassionate. Each name points toward something true. Each becomes a doorway through which the heart may enter reverence and wonder.

And yet, beyond every sacred name, God simply is.

The contemplative tradition gently reminds us that no description fully contains the Divine. No word captures the endless mystery of the One who holds all existence within Himself. And so there comes a moment in prayer when the soul stops grasping for explanations and simply rests before the reality of God’s presence. Not trying to define Him. Not trying to fully comprehend Him. Only turning toward Him in love.

This same simplicity is invited inwardly toward yourself as well. You do not need to endlessly sort through every quality, every flaw, every spiritual condition in order to approach God rightly. Beneath all the complexity of personality and experience is the quiet mystery that you exist at all. And your being itself already rests inside the Being of God.

The old writer speaks with awe here: everything you are comes from God continuously. Your life is not separate from the sustaining presence of divine Love. Even before you became aware of yourself, your existence was already held within the eternal heart of God. You were never self-created. Never self-sustained. Every breath is received moment by moment as gift.

And so contemplation becomes an act of profound trust. Instead of exhausting yourself through endless spiritual striving, you begin offering your whole existence back to the One from whom it came. Quietly. Simply. Lovingly.

The beautiful paradox is that this simplicity does not impoverish the soul — it nourishes it. When the mind stops scattering itself across endless analysis and restless seeking, the deeper parts of the soul begin to fill with a subtler wisdom. The old text describes the spiritual senses overflowing like wine, not through force or intellectual effort, but through grace. There are forms of knowing that cannot be manufactured through analysis. They arrive softly, like light spreading across still water.

This contemplative wisdom does not reject thought, learning, or reflection. It simply recognizes their limits. There are depths of God that can only be received through love, silence, surrender, and inward stillness. The soul begins to taste a wisdom too gentle for argument and too spacious for certainty.

And from this hidden communion, love naturally flows outward.

The old writer says that the whole law of God is fulfilled in love. Not merely through outward obedience, but through the inward knitting together of the soul with God Himself. The person who learns to rest deeply in divine love slowly becomes softer, steadier, more gracious toward others. What happens inwardly begins to shape the outward life without strain or performance.

The soul becomes peaceful because it has stopped trying to possess God.

And instead has allowed itself to be quietly possessed by Love.

### Breath Prayer

> **Inhale:** *You are*
>
> **Exhale:** *And I rest in You*
