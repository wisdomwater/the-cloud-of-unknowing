## 33 — The Holy Darkness Beyond Understanding

> *There is a kind of knowing that comes only when certainty falls away.*
>
> *A quiet darkness.*
>
> *A hidden fullness the mind cannot hold.*

The contemplative path eventually leads the soul into a strange and holy place.

A place where the ordinary ways of understanding no longer seem to work.

The ancient writer calls it “nothing.”

Nowhere.

A cloud of unknowing.

And at first, this sounds unsettling. The mind naturally wants clarity, definition, certainty, and direction. It wants something solid to grasp. Something it can organize, explain, and possess.

But God cannot be reduced into an object the mind controls.

The deeper the soul moves into contemplation, the more it begins realizing that every image, every concept, every explanation eventually reaches its limit.

And beyond that limit waits mystery.

Not empty.

Not absent.

But alive beyond comprehension.

The writer says something beautiful and difficult here: spiritually speaking, nowhere bodily becomes everywhere spiritually. In other words, when the mind stops trying to locate God as an object “somewhere,” the soul begins awakening to a Presence that cannot be confined by place at all.

This is why contemplative prayer often feels at first like entering darkness.

Not because God is absent.

But because the usual ways we navigate reality begin falling quiet.

The senses cannot grasp this Presence.

The imagination cannot fully picture it.

The intellect cannot master it.

And so the ordinary self feels disoriented.

The writer even says the outer self calls this experience “nothing.”

Because the ego does not know how to value what it cannot control.

And yet the inner self recognizes something entirely different.

Not nothing.

Everything.

For beneath this silence the soul begins encountering a deeper reality hidden underneath the noise of ordinary consciousness.

At first, however, this encounter can feel painful.

When people enter deep silence honestly, they often become newly aware of all they carry within themselves — old wounds, regrets, compulsions, fears, unfinished grief, hidden pride, restless desires, self-protection, shame.

The silence becomes like a mirror.

Not condemning.

Simply revealing.

And many people turn away at this point.

Not because contemplation has failed.

But because they begin encountering themselves without distraction.

The writer describes this experience almost like standing before a hidden inner purging. Some experience deep sorrow. Some feel dryness. Some feel temporarily abandoned. Others become discouraged because they expected spiritual sweetness and instead discovered inner conflict.

But the contemplative tradition gently reminds us: this too belongs to the path.

Not punishment.

Purification.

The slow uncovering of the heart.

And if the soul remains patient — not forcing, not fleeing, not grasping for immediate comfort — something gradually changes.

The inner heaviness begins softening.

The compulsive noise loses some of its grip.

Moments of unexpected peace begin appearing quietly between the thoughts.

Not because the soul has figured God out.

But because it has begun surrendering the need to.

This is the great paradox of contemplation: the closer the soul draws to God, the more it realizes God cannot be fully comprehended.

And strangely, this realization no longer feels frightening.

It becomes freeing.

The soul no longer needs to dominate mystery in order to trust it.

The writer says the deepest knowledge of God comes through unknowing.

Not ignorance in the ordinary sense.

But a sacred humility that stops trying to imprison the Infinite inside concepts small enough for the mind to manage.

Like standing beneath the night sky.

Like listening to music too beautiful to fully explain.

Like sitting silently beside someone deeply loved.

You do not possess the mystery.

You consent to it.

And in this surrender, the soul discovers a different kind of vision entirely.

Not sight through the eyes.

Not certainty through the intellect.

But quiet communion.

A hidden union born within love itself.
