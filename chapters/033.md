## 33 - The Holy Darkness Beyond Understanding

> *The soul draws nearest to God when it releases the need to possess Him through certainty.*

The contemplative path eventually leads you beyond the limits of ordinary understanding. The mind naturally seeks clarity, explanations, and conclusions it can hold onto. Yet no image, doctrine, or idea can fully contain the mystery of God. What begins as understanding gradually becomes wonder as you discover that the Divine is always greater than your thoughts about Him.

This can feel like entering a kind of holy darkness. Not because God is absent, but because the familiar ways of knowing begin to grow quiet. The imagination cannot picture this Presence, and the intellect cannot master it. What once felt like certainty gives way to mystery, inviting you to trust what cannot be controlled.

In this silence, you may also begin seeing yourself more clearly. Hidden fears, old wounds, restless desires, and long-buried grief can rise into awareness. This is not a sign that prayer has gone wrong. The light of Love is gently revealing what has been waiting to be healed, not to condemn you, but to bring you into greater wholeness.

If you remain patient, resisting the urge to flee or force an answer, something begins to soften within. The need for certainty loosens its grip. Moments of peace appear between the thoughts. You discover that faith is not the possession of perfect answers but the willingness to remain present within mystery.

This is the paradox at the heart of contemplation: the more deeply you encounter God, the more you realize He cannot be fully comprehended. Yet this realization becomes a source of freedom rather than fear. Like standing beneath a vast night sky or resting beside someone deeply loved, you no longer need to explain the mystery in order to trust it. Love itself becomes a way of seeing.
