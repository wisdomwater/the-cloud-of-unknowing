## 18 — The Simple Prayer of the Heart

> *The soul does not always need*
>
> *many words.*
>
> *Sometimes longing itself is prayer.*

There is a kind of prayer that becomes simpler as the soul matures.

Not because the heart becomes empty.

But because it becomes more honest.

In the beginning of the spiritual life, many words often feel necessary. The mind searches constantly for understanding. You try to explain yourself to God. You analyze your emotions, your failures, your desires, your questions. There is a natural place for this. Words can help awaken sincerity and attention.

But over time, contemplation draws the soul beneath explanation.

Toward something quieter.

More direct.

More whole.

The ancient contemplatives noticed that deep prayer often gathers itself into very simple movements of the heart. A single inward cry can sometimes carry more truth than long speeches spoken without attention.

Not because God prefers short prayers.

But because love simplifies.

When the heart is truly longing, it stops trying to impress anyone.

It becomes naked.

Honest.

Direct.

Like someone crying out in the middle of the night.

And often, the deepest prayers are not polished at all.

They rise from vulnerability.

From helplessness.

From longing too deep for language.

Sometimes a person can barely pray beyond:

God.

Mercy.

Help.

Stay.

And yet these small prayers may carry immense depth when they rise sincerely from within.

The ancient writer speaks of using simple words like “God” and “sin,” not as magical formulas, but as ways of gathering the whole heart into a single movement of attention. One word becomes a doorway. A place where the scattered mind slowly returns to simplicity again.

For much of contemplation involves learning how to stop fragmenting yourself endlessly.

The mind constantly divides reality into categories, arguments, measurements, comparisons, judgments, and anxieties. It wants to analyze every emotion and solve every uncertainty. But in deep prayer, the soul begins resting beneath this constant mental activity.

Not because thinking is bad.

But because God is greater than thought.

And love reaches places the intellect alone cannot touch.

So the contemplative learns to become simple before God.

Not simplistic.

Simple.

Like clear water.

Like open hands.

Like quiet breathing.

And this simplicity slowly changes the way you hold both your brokenness and your longing.

Instead of obsessing over every individual flaw, every passing emotion, every spiritual imperfection, you begin bringing your whole self honestly into the presence of Love. You stop dissecting yourself endlessly.

Not because your wounds are unimportant.

But because healing happens more deeply through surrender than through constant self-analysis.

Likewise, your longing for goodness becomes simpler too.

You stop chasing spiritual achievements one by one.

You stop trying to collect virtues like accomplishments.

You begin realizing that if you truly rest in God, everything else gradually flows from there.

Patience.

Compassion.

Humility.

Mercy.

Peace.

These are not separate trophies to acquire through spiritual ambition. They are fruits slowly growing from deeper communion.

And this realization becomes deeply restful.

Because it means you no longer have to hold your spiritual life together through anxiety and control.

You can simply keep returning.

Again and again.

Toward God.

Toward Love.

Toward the quiet center beneath all the noise.

The contemplative path does ask for perseverance. Not rigid perfection, but steady returning. The heart continues turning toward God in both strength and weakness, in clarity and confusion, in health and exhaustion alike.

Sometimes prayer feels alive and luminous.

Other times it feels dry and heavy.

Sometimes silence feels nourishing.

Other times it feels uncomfortable and restless.

But the invitation remains the same:

keep turning inward toward Love.

And do this gently.

For the contemplative life is not meant to crush the body or overwhelm the nervous system. The ancient writer speaks wisely about caring for your physical health, resting when needed, and accepting limitations with humility. Even suffering itself can become prayer when held patiently before God.

You do not need to force holiness into existence.

You do not need to drive yourself mercilessly toward spiritual perfection.

Love grows more quietly than that.

Like roots deepening beneath the earth.

Like dawn arriving slowly over the horizon.

So let your prayer become simple.

Let your heart become honest.

And trust that even the smallest sincere turning toward God is already held within infinite mercy.

## Breath Prayer

> Breath in: *Gather my scattered heart*
>
> Breath out: *Into simple Love*
