## 18 — The Simple Prayer of the Heart

> *The soul does not always need many words. Sometimes longing itself is prayer.*

There is a kind of prayer that grows simpler as the soul matures. Not because the heart becomes empty, but because it becomes more honest.

In the beginning of the spiritual life, many words often feel necessary. The mind searches constantly for understanding. You try to explain yourself to God, to sort through your emotions, failures, desires, fears, and questions. There is a natural goodness in this. Words can awaken sincerity. They can help gather attention and open the heart toward what is holy.

But over time, contemplation slowly draws the soul beneath explanation. Toward something quieter. More direct. More whole.

The ancient contemplatives noticed that deep prayer often gathers itself into very simple movements of the heart. A single inward cry may sometimes carry more truth than long speeches spoken without attention. Not because God prefers short prayers, but because love itself simplifies.

When the heart truly longs for God, it stops trying to impress anyone. It becomes naked. Honest. Direct.

Like someone crying out softly in the middle of the night.

And often, the deepest prayers are not polished at all. They rise from vulnerability, helplessness, and longing too deep for language. Sometimes a person can barely pray beyond a few quiet words:

God.

Mercy.

Help.

Stay.

And yet these small prayers may carry immense depth when they rise sincerely from within.

The ancient writer speaks of using simple words like “God” and “sin,” not as magical formulas, but as ways of gathering the scattered heart into a single movement of attention. One small word becomes a doorway through which the soul quietly returns to simplicity again.

For much of contemplation involves learning how to stop fragmenting yourself endlessly.

The mind divides reality into categories, arguments, measurements, comparisons, judgments, and anxieties. It wants to analyze every emotion and solve every uncertainty. But in deep prayer, the soul slowly learns to rest beneath this constant mental activity.

Not because thinking is bad. But because God is greater than thought. And love reaches places the intellect alone cannot touch.

So the contemplative gradually learns to become simple before God. Not simplistic. Simple. Like clear water. Like open hands. Like quiet breathing.

And this simplicity slowly changes the way you hold both your brokenness and your longing. Instead of obsessing over every flaw, every passing emotion, and every spiritual imperfection, you begin bringing your whole self honestly into the presence of Love. You stop dissecting yourself endlessly.

Not because your wounds are unimportant.

But because healing often happens more deeply through surrender than through constant self-analysis.

And your longing for goodness becomes simpler as well. You stop chasing spiritual achievements one by one. You stop trying to collect virtues like accomplishments earned through effort and anxiety. Gradually, you begin realizing that when the soul truly rests in God, everything else slowly grows from that deeper communion.

Patience. Compassion. Humility. Mercy. Peace.

These are not trophies to acquire through spiritual ambition. They are fruits that quietly grow from abiding in Love.

And this realization becomes deeply restful.

Because it means you no longer have to hold your spiritual life together through fear, control, and endless striving. You can simply keep returning.

Again and again, you return. Toward God. Toward Love. Toward the quiet center beneath the noise.

The contemplative path does ask for perseverance. Not rigid perfection, but steady returning. The heart continues turning toward God in strength and weakness alike, in clarity and confusion, in joy and exhaustion.

Sometimes prayer feels luminous and alive. Other times it feels dry and heavy. Sometimes silence feels nourishing. Other times it feels restless and uncomfortable. But the invitation remains the same: keep turning inward toward Love.

And do this gently.

For the contemplative life is not meant to crush the body or overwhelm the nervous system. The ancient writer speaks wisely about caring for physical health, resting when needed, and accepting human limitations with humility. Even suffering itself can become prayer when it is held patiently before God.

You do not need to force holiness into existence. You do not need to drive yourself mercilessly toward spiritual perfection.

Love grows more quietly than that. Like roots deepening beneath the earth. Like dawn arriving slowly over the horizon.

So let your prayer become simple. Let your heart become honest. And trust that even the smallest sincere turning toward God is already being held within infinite mercy.

### Breath Prayer

> **Inhale:** *Gather my scattered heart*
>
> **Exhale:** *Into simple Love*
