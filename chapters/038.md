## 38 — The Offering of Your Whole Being

> *Love becomes simplest when the soul stops dividing itself.*

There is a way of praying that slowly gathers the scattered pieces of the soul back into wholeness. Much of life pulls the mind outward into endless analysis, comparison, striving, and self-consciousness. Even spirituality can become fragmented when we are constantly measuring ourselves, evaluating our progress, or trying to construct perfect thoughts about God. But contemplation invites something far gentler: the quiet offering of your whole being just as it is.

The old writer speaks of offering your “substance” to God — not merely your thoughts, emotions, talents, or spiritual efforts, but your very existence itself. Before all your qualities and experiences, before all your strengths and weaknesses, there is the simple reality that you are. This naked being is already a gift flowing continually from God. Every breath, every moment of awareness, every movement of life rests inside the sustaining presence of divine Love.

And so the contemplative path becomes less about examining every detail of yourself and more about returning to the simple center beneath all those details. You may notice beautiful qualities within yourself — compassion, longing, wisdom, tenderness, grief, hope, weakness, even your capacity to love God — and all these can awaken gratitude. Yet the soul eventually learns to gather all these many qualities into one quiet offering. “All that I am comes from You,” the heart whispers. “And all that I am returns to You.”

This kind of prayer carries within it a hidden love for others as well. When the soul rests deeply in God, it does not become isolated or indifferent. True contemplation widens the heart. The one who quietly offers themselves to God in love is, in some mysterious way, carrying others within that offering too. Love ceases to be narrow and possessive. It becomes spacious enough to hold the suffering, longing, and healing of the world.

The writer compares this to the way Christ offered Himself completely — not selectively, not for only a few, but wholly and universally in love. In contemplation, the soul learns this same movement of surrender. You stop trying to preserve yourself through constant self-focus and instead begin dissolving gently into a deeper communion that embraces both God and neighbor together.

And this is why the contemplative tradition so often returns to simplicity. The mind believes that spiritual progress comes through accumulating more insight, more techniques, more understanding. But there are moments when the soul grows most deeply not by adding more, but by becoming less divided. Less scattered. Less consumed with examining every movement within itself.

You do not need to force yourself into spiritual complexity.

You only need to remain quietly turned toward God.

And over time, the soul discovers that this simple, loving attention nourishes it more deeply than restless searching ever could.

### Breath Prayer

> **Inhale:** *All that I am is Yours*
>
> **Exhale:** *Gather me into Your love*
