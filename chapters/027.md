## 27 - The Fire That Judges Others

> *Love does not grow through superiority.*

It is easy to mistake judgment for wisdom and certainty for spiritual maturity. When you feel unsettled, wounded, or afraid, focusing on the faults of others can create a temporary sense of control. The mind divides the world into those who are right and those who are wrong, those who are awake and those who are blind. Yet this habit rarely brings peace to the soul.

The contemplative path invites a different response. Not the abandonment of truth, but the practice of holding truth with humility. As you grow in self-awareness, you begin to see how complex every human life truly is. The struggles, fears, wounds, and contradictions you discover within yourself help you meet others with greater patience and compassion.

Love softens the need to judge quickly. You become less interested in proving yourself right and more interested in understanding deeply. Discernment remains important, but it is no longer driven by outrage or superiority. Instead, it is guided by humility, remembering how much remains unseen and unknown.

This humility also protects you from the temptation of spiritual pride. The more certain you become of your own special insight, the easier it is to lose touch with compassion. Contemplation continually returns you to groundedness, reminding you that wisdom grows best in a heart willing to listen, learn, and remain teachable.

As you draw nearer to God, harshness begins to loosen its grip. You no longer feel responsible for correcting every person or carrying the burden of judging every situation. Instead, you begin to see what Love sees: fragile, unfinished people doing their best to find their way. And within that shared humanity, mercy quietly grows.

**Breath Prayer**

> Inhale: *Soften my harshness*
>
> Exhale: *Teach me compassionate wisdom*
