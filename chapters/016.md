## 16 — Let Love Work Within You

> *You do not create the deepest prayer.*
>
> *You consent to it.*
>
> *You allow yourself to be carried.*

There comes a point in the contemplative life when you begin realizing that the deepest movements of prayer are not something you accomplish by your own effort.

At first, this can feel unsettling.

The mind wants instructions.

Methods.

Guarantees.

It wants to know exactly how spiritual transformation happens and how to control the process. But contemplation slowly loosens this need for mastery. The soul begins learning that the most sacred movements unfold beyond the reach of force.

Love itself becomes the teacher.

And strangely, the more deeply you enter this path, the less certain you become about your own ability to explain it.

Not because nothing is happening.

But because what is happening belongs more to direct experience than to language.

The ancient contemplatives often describe this with remarkable humility. They speak as people who have touched mystery just enough to know it cannot be possessed. They stop trying to define every movement of grace. They become more comfortable standing quietly before what cannot be fully understood.

And this humility protects the soul.

For pride always wants to take ownership of spiritual experience. The ego longs to feel advanced, special, pure, or enlightened. It wants to build an identity out of holiness itself.

But contemplation gently dismantles these illusions.

You begin realizing that even your longing for God is already grace moving within you.

Even your desire to pray is not fully self-created.

Something deeper is calling you from within.

Something wiser than your ordinary thinking mind.

And because of this, the contemplative path becomes less about achieving and more about consenting.

You stop trying to force divine union into existence.

You stop trying to manufacture inner peace through sheer effort.

Instead, you begin learning how to cooperate with the quiet movement already unfolding inside you.

This requires trust.

And patience.

And a willingness to become still enough to notice what grace is already doing beneath the surface.

The ancient writer offers a beautiful image here: be the house, and let God be the one who dwells within it. Be the tree, and let Love shape you like a craftsman shaping wood.

There is something deeply healing in this surrender.

Especially for those who have spent much of life striving endlessly to fix themselves.

Many people approach spirituality carrying hidden exhaustion. They believe they must constantly improve themselves, purify themselves, perfect themselves, or earn closeness with God through endless effort. Prayer becomes another form of pressure layered upon an already weary nervous system.

But contemplative prayer moves differently.

It invites rest.

Not passivity.

Not avoidance.

But a deeper kind of trust.

A trust that God is already nearer than your thoughts.

Already working within you more tenderly than you realize.

Already drawing you toward healing in hidden ways.

And this changes how you relate to distraction, weakness, and struggle.

You no longer panic when thoughts arise.

You no longer collapse into shame every time your mind wanders.

You begin understanding that the path is not about becoming flawlessly silent. It is about gently returning beneath the noise again and again.

And over time, this returning becomes simpler.

Less dramatic.

Less forceful.

More like yielding than conquering.

The soul gradually learns how to stop interfering with grace quite so much.

And there is another freedom here as well:

you no longer need to understand everything clearly before moving forward.

The contemplative path often unfolds through intuition, longing, and quiet inward attraction long before the intellect fully comprehends what is happening. You may feel drawn toward silence without knowing why. Drawn toward stillness. Drawn toward prayer that has no words.

This is alright.

You do not need to grasp the whole mystery.

You only need enough willingness to keep turning toward it.

And slowly, almost imperceptibly, Love begins reshaping the inner world from the inside out.

Not through force.

But through presence.

## Breath Prayer

> Breath in: *Teach me how to yield*
>
> Breath out: *To the work of Love*
