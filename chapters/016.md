## 16 — Let Love Work Within You

> *You do not create the deepest prayer. You consent to it. You allow yourself to be carried.*

There comes a point in the contemplative life when you begin realizing that the deepest movements of prayer are not something you accomplish through effort alone. At first, this realization can feel unsettling because the mind naturally longs for clarity, structure, and control. It wants instructions to follow, methods to master, and guarantees about how transformation will unfold. But contemplation slowly loosens this need for certainty and mastery. The soul begins learning that the most sacred movements often happen beyond the reach of force.

Love itself becomes the teacher.

And strangely, the deeper you travel along this path, the less certain you become about your ability to fully explain what is happening within you. Not because nothing is happening, but because what unfolds in contemplation belongs more to direct experience than to language. The ancient contemplatives spoke of these things with remarkable humility. They wrote as people who had touched mystery just enough to understand it could never be possessed.

So they stopped trying to define every movement of grace. They became more willing to stand quietly before what could not be fully understood.

And this humility protects the soul.

For pride always wants to claim ownership of spiritual experience. The ego longs to feel advanced, enlightened, pure, or spiritually accomplished. It wants to build an identity even out of holiness itself. But contemplation gently dismantles these illusions. Over time, you begin realizing that even your longing for God is already grace moving within you. Even your desire to pray did not arise entirely from your own effort.

Something deeper is already calling you.

Something wiser than the ordinary thinking mind.

And because of this, the contemplative path becomes less about achieving and more about consenting. You stop trying to force divine union into existence. You stop trying to manufacture peace through sheer discipline and self-effort. Instead, you begin learning how to cooperate with the quiet movement already unfolding beneath the surface of your life.

This requires trust.

And patience.

And a willingness to become still enough to notice what grace is already doing within you.

The ancient writer offers a beautiful image for this surrender: be the house, and allow God to dwell within it. Be the tree, and allow Love to shape you as a craftsman slowly shapes wood. There is something deeply healing in this vision, especially for those who have spent much of life striving endlessly to fix themselves.

Many people carry hidden exhaustion into their spiritual lives. They believe they must constantly improve themselves, purify themselves, perfect themselves, or earn closeness with God through endless effort. Prayer itself becomes another form of pressure placed upon an already weary nervous system.

But contemplative prayer moves differently.

It invites rest.

Not passivity. Not avoidance. But a deeper kind of trust—a trust that God is already nearer than your thoughts, already working within you more tenderly than you realize, already drawing you toward healing in hidden ways.

And this changes how you begin relating to distraction, weakness, and struggle.

You no longer panic when thoughts arise. You no longer collapse into shame each time the mind wanders. Gradually, you begin understanding that the contemplative path is not about becoming flawlessly silent or spiritually perfect. It is about gently returning beneath the noise again and again.

And over time, this returning becomes simpler. Less dramatic. Less forceful. More like yielding than conquering.

The soul slowly learns how to stop interfering with grace quite so much.

And there is another freedom hidden here as well: you no longer need to understand everything clearly before moving forward. The contemplative path often unfolds through longing, intuition, and quiet inward attraction long before the intellect fully comprehends what is happening. You may feel drawn toward silence without being able to explain why. Drawn toward stillness. Drawn toward prayer that no longer depends upon words.

This is alright.

You do not need to grasp the whole mystery.

You only need enough willingness to keep turning toward it.

And slowly, almost imperceptibly, Love begins reshaping your inner world from the inside out.

Not through force.

But through presence.

### Breath Prayer

> **Inhale:** *Teach me how to yield*
>
> **Exhale:** *To the work of Love*
