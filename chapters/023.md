## 23 — The Hidden Center Within

> *The inward journey is not escape.*
>
> *It is a quiet returning*
>
> *to the deepest place of love.*

One of the great dangers on the contemplative path is misunderstanding what inwardness truly means.

When people first hear about “going within,” they sometimes imagine spirituality as a kind of escape from ordinary life, the body, emotions, or the world itself. The imagination becomes fascinated with hidden experiences, altered states, secret wisdom, or dramatic inner phenomena.

And without realizing it, the person slowly drifts away from groundedness.

The ancient contemplatives speak carefully here because they understood how easily spiritual longing can become tangled with fantasy, ego, anxiety, or psychological strain. A sincere desire for God can become distorted when the mind tries to force mystical experiences instead of resting humbly in love.

This happens more easily than many people realize.

Especially for sensitive people.

Especially for wounded people longing for certainty.

Especially for those who carry hidden desperation beneath their spirituality.

The mind begins turning endlessly inward, not with peaceful awareness, but with obsessive intensity. Every feeling becomes overexamined. Every inward sensation becomes spiritually charged. The imagination starts producing elaborate meanings and interpretations.

And eventually the soul becomes exhausted.

Not because contemplation is harmful.

But because contemplation was never meant to become psychological self-consumption.

True inward prayer is much simpler than that.

It is not collapsing into yourself.

It is not fixating on your thoughts.

It is not becoming trapped in endless spiritual analysis.

The inward journey the mystics speak of is actually a movement beyond self-absorption.

A quiet descent beneath the noise.

Beneath performance.

Beneath compulsive thinking.

Beneath the restless need to control spiritual outcomes.

And there, in that hidden simplicity, the soul slowly discovers a deeper center held gently within God.

This is why humility matters so deeply.

The ancient writer warns about spiritual pride disguised as mystical ambition. Sometimes people believe they are ready for deep contemplative work simply because they are fascinated by spiritual ideas or unusual experiences. They abandon ordinary disciplines too quickly. They lose touch with grounded wisdom, wise counsel, rest, embodiment, and patience.

But genuine contemplation makes a person softer, steadier, and more human.

Not inflated.

Not detached from reality.

Not secretly convinced of their spiritual superiority.

The deeper the soul moves into God, the more ordinary and compassionate it often becomes.

More patient.

More gentle.

More able to live quietly without needing to appear spiritually impressive.

This is why the contemplative path asks for such careful tenderness toward your own mind and body.

Do not force yourself inward violently.

Do not treat your nervous system harshly in the name of spirituality.

Do not confuse exhaustion or intensity with enlightenment.

God is not hiding behind psychological strain.

Love is not found through inner aggression.

The path is quieter than that.

Softer.

More grounded.

More merciful.

The soul does not reach God by climbing into spiritual spectacle.

It simply relaxes, little by little, into honest presence.

And in that quiet honesty, something beautiful begins to happen.

The inner world becomes less crowded.

Less frantic.

Less consumed with proving, grasping, or escaping.

The heart settles.

Awareness softens.

And beneath all the noise, the soul begins discovering the hidden center where God has been waiting all along.

Not far away.

Not hidden behind secret knowledge.

But gently present within the silent depths of love itself.
