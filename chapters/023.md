## 23 — The Hidden Center Within

> *The inward journey is not escape, but a quiet return to Love.*

One of the great misunderstandings surrounding the contemplative life is the belief that inwardness means withdrawal from reality. When people first hear about “going within,” they sometimes imagine spirituality as an escape from ordinary life, the body, relationships, or the pain of being human. The imagination becomes captivated by hidden experiences, altered states, secret wisdom, or dramatic inner phenomena, and without realizing it, the soul slowly drifts away from groundedness and simplicity.

The ancient contemplatives spoke carefully about this because they understood how easily sincere spiritual longing can become tangled with fantasy, ego, fear, or emotional exhaustion. A genuine desire for God can slowly become distorted when the mind begins striving after mystical experiences rather than resting humbly in love itself.

And this happens more easily than many people realize. Especially for tender and sensitive souls. Especially for wounded people who quietly ache for certainty. Especially for those carrying hidden desperation beneath their spirituality.

The mind can begin turning inward with restless intensity rather than peaceful awareness. Every feeling becomes overexamined. Every inward sensation seems spiritually significant. The imagination starts attaching elaborate meanings to ordinary experiences, and little by little the soul becomes trapped inside its own constant analysis.

Eventually, exhaustion follows. Not because contemplation itself is harmful, but because contemplation was never meant to become psychological self-consumption. True inward prayer is far gentler than that.

It is not collapsing endlessly into yourself. It is not obsessing over thoughts or becoming trapped in ceaseless spiritual interpretation. The inward journey spoken of by the mystics is actually a movement beyond self-absorption. It is a quiet descent beneath the noise of performance, beneath compulsive thinking, beneath the anxious need to control spiritual outcomes or secure certainty.

And there, beneath all the striving, the soul slowly discovers something deeper and more stable — a hidden center quietly held within God.

This is why humility matters so deeply along the contemplative path. The ancient writer warns gently about spiritual pride disguised as mystical ambition. Sometimes a person becomes fascinated with spiritual ideas or unusual experiences and assumes this fascination alone makes them ready for deep contemplative work. They may abandon ordinary rhythms too quickly. They may neglect grounded wisdom, wise counsel, embodiment, rest, patience, or simple human tenderness.

But genuine contemplation does not make a person inflated or detached from reality. It makes them softer. Steadier. More deeply human.

The deeper the soul moves into God, the less concerned it becomes with appearing spiritually impressive. Love quietly removes the need to perform holiness. A contemplative heart often becomes more patient, more compassionate, more gentle with weakness — both in itself and in others. It learns how to live quietly without needing constant affirmation that it is progressing spiritually.

This is why the contemplative path asks for such tenderness toward your own mind and body. You do not need to force yourself inward violently. You do not need to strain your nervous system in the name of spirituality. Exhaustion is not enlightenment. Intensity is not always wisdom. God is not hiding behind psychological strain or hidden inside inner aggression.

The path is quieter than that. Softer. More merciful.

The soul does not reach God through spiritual spectacle. It simply relaxes, little by little, into honest presence. And within that honesty, something beautiful begins to unfold. The inner world becomes less crowded and frantic. The heart no longer feels consumed with grasping, proving, escaping, or endlessly searching for certainty.

Instead, the soul slowly settles. Awareness softens. Silence becomes less frightening.

And beneath all the noise, you begin discovering the hidden center where God has been waiting all along. Not far away. Not hidden behind secret knowledge. But gently present within the silent depths of love itself.
