## 22 — Love Beyond Feeling

> *The deepest love does not depend*
>
> *on constant sweetness.*
>
> *It remains even in quietness.*

Many people begin the spiritual journey searching for an experience.

They long to feel close to God. They hope for peace, comfort, warmth, reassurance, clarity, or moments of overwhelming beauty. And sometimes these gifts truly do appear. A person may feel sudden tenderness in prayer. Tears may come unexpectedly. The heart may soften into deep stillness or joy.

These moments are real.

They matter.

But the ancient contemplatives gently remind us not to build our faith upon them.

For spiritual maturity slowly teaches the soul to love God beyond emotional reward.

This is one of the quiet thresholds of contemplation.

At first, the heart naturally delights in sweetness. It feels encouraged by moments of comfort and discouraged when they fade. This is deeply human. We all long to feel held. We all seek signs that we are loved and guided.

But over time, prayer becomes less dependent on emotional reassurance.

Love grows steadier.

Simpler.

More rooted.

The ancient writer says something profoundly freeing here: the heart of the spiritual life is not spiritual sensation, but a willing and loving consent toward God.

A quiet inner yes.

This matters more than visions.

More than feelings.

More than emotional intensity.

For feelings rise and fall like weather. Some days the soul feels full of warmth. Other days prayer may feel dry, distracted, or hidden behind clouds. But beneath all these changing experiences, love can still remain.

And this hidden faithfulness is precious.

The soul slowly learns how to stay near God without demanding constant comfort.

Not because comfort is wrong.

But because love itself becomes enough.

This does not mean you must reject moments of sweetness when they come. Receive them gently. Be grateful for them. Let them nourish your tired heart when needed.

But do not cling to them.

Do not build your entire spiritual life around recreating them.

For when we become too attached to spiritual feelings, we may unknowingly begin loving the feeling more than God. We may panic when peace disappears. We may assume we are failing whenever prayer feels ordinary again.

But contemplation invites a deeper stability.

A love that remains even when the emotions quiet down.

A trust that survives uncertainty.

A willingness to sit quietly before God without needing immediate consolation.

This kind of love becomes chaste in the deepest sense of the word.

Pure.

Not possessive.

Not demanding.

Not bargaining for emotional reward.

Simply available.

And strangely, this kind of surrendered love often creates a gentler nervous system as well. The soul no longer swings so violently between spiritual excitement and spiritual despair. Prayer becomes less frantic. Less pressured.

More like resting.

More like companionship.

The ancient writer also speaks wisely about how different people are given different experiences along the path. Some people receive deep emotional comfort often. Others encounter it rarely. Some feel tenderness in the body while praying. Others walk mostly through quiet faith without strong sensation.

Neither path is superior.

God tends each soul differently.

Some hearts need comfort in order to endure suffering. Some bodies are weary and fragile and need reassurance along the way. Others are strengthened more through hidden trust and simplicity.

There is no need to compare your path to someone else’s.

The spiritual life is not a competition of experiences.

Love unfolds uniquely within every person.

And perhaps this is one of the hardest lessons for modern people to accept:

You do not need extraordinary spiritual experiences in order to be deeply close to God.

The quiet willingness to remain present in love is already holy.

The simple desire to belong to God is already prayer.

Even your longing counts.

Even your unfinishedness.

Even your quiet returning after distraction.

The deepest transformation often happens silently, beneath the surface, where no dramatic experience can fully measure it.

And there, beneath all changing feelings, love continues its slow and holy work.

## Breath Prayer

> Breath in: *Teach me steady love*
>
> Breath out: *Beyond every feeling*
