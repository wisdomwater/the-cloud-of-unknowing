## 22 — Love Beyond Feeling

> *The deepest love remains even when sweetness fades.*

Many people begin the spiritual journey hoping for an experience of God that feels unmistakable. They long for peace, comfort, warmth, clarity, or moments when the heart seems flooded with beauty and reassurance. And sometimes these gifts truly are given. A person may feel sudden tenderness while praying. Tears may rise unexpectedly. The soul may settle into a stillness so gentle and luminous that for a moment everything feels held within divine love.

These moments are real. They matter. They can nourish the weary heart in profound ways.

Yet the ancient contemplatives gently caution us not to build the foundation of our spiritual life upon these experiences alone. Emotional sweetness is a gift, but it is not the deepest measure of love. Over time, the soul slowly learns that spiritual maturity is not found in constantly feeling close to God, but in remaining turned toward God even when the feelings themselves grow quiet.

This is one of the hidden thresholds of contemplation.

In the beginning, the heart naturally clings to consolation. We feel encouraged when prayer feels warm and alive, and discouraged when it becomes dry or distracted. This is deeply human. We all long to feel held. We all seek reassurance that we are loved, guided, and not alone along the way.

But slowly, if the soul remains patient, love begins to deepen beyond emotional reward.

Prayer becomes steadier. Simpler. More rooted in quiet consent.

The ancient writer speaks of something profoundly freeing here: the heart of the spiritual life is not extraordinary sensation, but a sincere and willing turning toward God. A quiet inward yes offered again and again, even when nothing dramatic seems to happen.

This quiet consent matters more than visions. More than feelings. More than emotional intensity.

For emotions rise and fall like changing weather. Some days the heart feels tender and full of light. Other days prayer may seem hidden behind clouds, ordinary and dry. Yet beneath all these changing experiences, love itself can continue to remain.

And this hidden faithfulness is precious in the eyes of God.

The soul slowly learns how to stay near without demanding constant comfort. Not because comfort is wrong, but because love itself gradually becomes enough. The heart no longer approaches prayer only to receive reassurance. It begins to rest more deeply in simple companionship with the Divine.

This does not mean you must reject moments of sweetness when they come. Receive them gently. Give thanks for them. Let them strengthen and nourish you when your spirit feels tired. But do not cling to them too tightly or build your entire spiritual life around trying to recreate them.

For when we become overly attached to spiritual feelings, we may slowly begin loving the feeling more than God Himself. We may grow anxious when peace disappears or assume we are failing whenever prayer feels ordinary again. The contemplative path invites something steadier than this — a love that remains present even when emotional certainty fades.

A trust that survives quietness.

A willingness to remain before God without needing immediate consolation.

This kind of love becomes pure in a deep and hidden way. It is no longer bargaining for emotional reward or grasping for spiritual excitement. It becomes softer, freer, and more surrendered. Simply available to God as it is.

And strangely, this surrendered love often brings greater peace to the body as well. The nervous system no longer swings so violently between spiritual excitement and spiritual despair. Prayer becomes less pressured and frantic. More like resting beside someone who already knows and loves you completely.

The ancient contemplatives also remind us that each soul walks differently along this path. Some people receive deep emotional comfort often. Others experience very little sensation at all. Some feel tenderness strongly within the body during prayer. Others travel quietly through hidden trust without dramatic experiences.

Neither path is superior. God tends each soul according to its need.

Some hearts require comfort in order to endure suffering. Some bodies are weary and fragile and need reassurance along the way. Others are strengthened more deeply through simplicity, silence, and hidden faithfulness. There is no need to compare your journey to another person’s. The spiritual life is not a competition of experiences.

Love unfolds uniquely within every soul.

And perhaps this is one of the hardest truths for modern people to accept: you do not need extraordinary spiritual experiences in order to be deeply close to God. The quiet willingness to remain turned toward Love is already holy. The simple desire to belong to God is already a form of prayer.

Even your longing counts. Even your unfinishedness. Even your quiet returning after distraction.

Much of the deepest transformation happens silently beneath the surface, where no feeling or dramatic experience can fully measure it. And there, underneath all changing emotions, love continues its slow and holy work within you.
