## 35 — Holy Desire Never Truly Leaves

> *The soul is shaped by longing.*
>
> *Even absence can deepen love.*
>
> *Grace often grows quietly beneath the surface.*

The contemplative path cannot be forced.

It cannot be manufactured through technique, intelligence, or spiritual ambition.

At its deepest level, contemplation is always response.

A response to grace.

A response to the quiet drawing of God already moving within the soul.

And because of this, the ancient writer says something deeply comforting: not everyone is called into this way in the same manner. Some may admire contemplative prayer from a distance without feeling inwardly drawn toward it. Others may feel an unmistakable pull toward silence, stillness, and loving awareness that continues returning again and again throughout their life.

This quiet returning matters.

The writer describes it almost like a hidden fire beneath the surface of the soul — a small but persistent desire for God that keeps reawakening no matter how distracted, discouraged, or spiritually dry a person becomes.

Not constant emotional intensity.

Not dramatic mystical experiences.

But longing.

A simple inward ache for deeper communion.

And this longing itself becomes one of the signs that grace is quietly at work.

The writer is careful here because the human mind can easily confuse curiosity with calling. Sometimes people become fascinated with mystical ideas simply because they feel unusual, poetic, or intellectually stimulating. But contemplation is not spiritual entertainment.

It asks something deeper.

It asks for surrender.

Patience.

Honesty.

Perseverance through silence.

A willingness to remain before God even when no immediate comfort appears.

And this is often where the real transformation begins.

For the contemplative path includes seasons when the felt closeness of God seems to fade. Prayer feels dry. Silence feels empty. The heart that once felt warm and open now feels distracted, tired, or distant.

Many people become frightened during these seasons.

They assume they have failed spiritually.

Or that God has withdrawn in disappointment.

But the writer gently says otherwise.

Sometimes grace withdraws its sweetness for a time so the soul does not become prideful or dependent upon spiritual feelings alone. Sometimes the silence deepens longing. Sometimes absence stretches the heart wider so it can receive more deeply later.

The loss itself becomes part of the preparation.

And strangely, when the sense of God’s nearness eventually returns, it is often received with greater humility, tenderness, and gratitude than before.

The soul begins learning that contemplation is not about controlling spiritual experiences.

It is about consenting to relationship.

Love matures slowly.

Not through constant intensity.

But through faithfulness across changing seasons.

This is true in every deep relationship.

There are moments of closeness.

Moments of silence.

Moments of joy.

Moments of uncertainty.

And yet love continues quietly growing beneath them all.

The writer says holy desires grow stronger through delay.

This is such a beautiful truth.

What is shallow usually fades when fulfillment is postponed.

But what is real deepens.

Like roots growing downward during winter.

Like seeds hidden beneath dark soil.

The longing itself becomes part of the transformation.

Over time, the soul begins realizing that even the ache for God is already a form of communion. The desire to love God more deeply is itself evidence that Love has already begun its work within you.

And this realization softens something important.

You stop needing to judge yourself so harshly.

You stop demanding constant spiritual certainty.

You stop measuring prayer by emotional intensity alone.

Instead, you begin trusting the quieter movements of grace.

The returning.

The longing.

The willingness to begin again.

Again and again.

The ancient writer closes with tenderness and humility. He does not claim perfection. He simply offers companionship along the path and entrusts the rest to God.

And perhaps this is the deepest wisdom hidden inside the contemplative life:

The soul is not perfected through force.

It is slowly ripened through love.

Through holy desire.

Through quiet returning.

Through grace patiently drawing the heart toward deeper peace, even through seasons of darkness and delay.

And if the longing remains alive within you, however faintly, then the journey is still unfolding.

Love has not left you.

Not even now.
