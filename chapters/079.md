## 79 — Softly Toward God

> *Love grows best when it is gentle.*

Many people begin the spiritual journey with intensity.

They want transformation.

They want healing.

They want God.

And because the longing is sincere, they often assume that greater effort must produce greater results.

So they strain.

They force.

They push themselves harder.

The author offers a different path.

He repeatedly warns against spiritual violence.

Do not force the body.

Do not agitate the emotions.

Do not attempt to manufacture devotion.

Do not try to seize God through effort.

Instead, he says, learn to love softly.

This does not mean loving weakly.

It does not mean loving half-heartedly.

It means loving without strain.

The soul turns toward God with steadiness rather than force.

Like a flower turning toward sunlight.

Like a river flowing toward the sea.

Like a child resting in the presence of someone deeply trusted.

The movement is real.

The desire is real.

But it is not frantic.

Many of us have learned to approach life through effort. We push through discomfort. We work harder. We solve problems. We overcome obstacles. These skills are useful in many areas of life.

But contemplative prayer asks something different of us.

Not conquest.

Not achievement.

Not control.

It asks for availability.

The author suggests that the deepest movements of prayer do not arise from emotional excitement or bodily intensity. They arise from a quiet willingness within the heart. The soul learns to consent rather than compel.

This is one reason contemplative traditions often speak about surrender.

Not because effort is bad.

But because there comes a point where effort can only carry us so far.

Love cannot be forced.

Trust cannot be forced.

Presence cannot be forced.

The more tightly we grasp, the more elusive these things become.

The author then turns to an important distinction between two kinds of spiritual experience.

Sometimes emotional warmth, sweetness, or excitement arises suddenly within us. Such experiences may be beautiful. They may even be gifts from God. But they can also arise from many other sources: personality, imagination, mood, memory, or simple biology.

For this reason, he advises caution.

Do not become attached to experiences.

Do not build your faith upon feelings.

Do not chase spiritual highs.

Instead, remain rooted in God Himself.

The deeper movement of prayer begins in the quiet center of the soul and flows outward. It is less dramatic but often more trustworthy. The fruit is not excitement but steadiness.

Not intensity but faithfulness.

Not ecstasy but love.

This wisdom feels especially relevant in a world that constantly rewards stimulation. We are surrounded by systems designed to capture attention, trigger emotion, and create immediate reactions.

Yet the deepest transformations often happen quietly.

A little more patience.

A little more compassion.

A little more surrender.

A little more trust.

These changes rarely make headlines.

Yet they slowly reshape a life.

The author repeatedly returns to a simple instruction: remain with the gentle movement toward God.

Do not become distracted by extraordinary experiences.

Do not become fascinated by visions, revelations, or spiritual sensations.

Do not become consumed by analyzing every interior event.

Simply remain faithful to love.

For when the heart is anchored in love, experiences can come and go without causing confusion.

Joy may come.

Dryness may come.

Comfort may come.

Uncertainty may come.

Yet beneath them all remains the same quiet orientation toward God.

This is the hidden strength of contemplative prayer.

It is not built upon feelings.

It is built upon willingness.

And willingness can remain even when feelings disappear.

Perhaps this is why the author places such emphasis on gentleness.

A forced spirituality eventually exhausts itself.

A gentle spirituality can endure for a lifetime.

For love grows most deeply when it is not pushed, but welcomed.

Not seized, but received.

Not driven, but allowed.

Softly.

Patiently.

One turning of the heart at a time.

### Breath Prayer

> **Inhale:** *Teach me to trust*
