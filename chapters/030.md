## 30 — The Nearness That Cannot Be Measured

> *God is not reached by distance.*
>
> *Love arrives before thought can travel.*
>
> *The heart moves where the body cannot.*

Many people begin the contemplative path imagining that God must somehow be far away.

Somewhere above.

Somewhere distant.

Somewhere hidden beyond the visible world.

And because the mind imagines God as far away, it often tries to reach Him through effort, intensity, or spiritual striving. The imagination stretches upward as though holiness were located in another place entirely.

But the deeper contemplative tradition gently loosens this idea.

For God is not reached through physical distance.

Not through climbing.

Not through forcing the mind upward.

Not through straining the imagination into heaven.

The ancient writer speaks carefully here because the human mind naturally turns spiritual language into physical images. We hear words like “lift up,” “ascend,” or “go inward,” and immediately the imagination begins constructing movement through space.

But contemplative prayer is not a journey across distance.

It is a surrender into Presence.

The soul does not travel toward God the way the body travels from one place to another. Love moves differently than the body moves. Desire reaches where thought cannot fully enter. The heart can rest in God even while sitting quietly in an ordinary room.

This is why the contemplatives say heaven is not truly far away.

Not spiritually.

For wherever genuine love awakens toward God, the soul already begins participating in that deeper reality. A quiet longing for God carries the heart farther than many restless spiritual efforts.

The old writer says the nearest way to heaven is run not by footsteps, but by desire.

And this remains deeply true.

A single sincere turning of the heart may open more than endless striving ever could.

A weary person whispering a quiet prayer in exhaustion may be nearer to God than someone desperately trying to manufacture spiritual intensity.

Because contemplation is not about producing extraordinary experiences.

It is about consenting to Love.

Resting inside mystery.

Allowing yourself to be gently drawn beyond the constant need to control, define, and achieve.

This is why contemplative prayer often feels strangely simple.

Nothing dramatic may appear to happen outwardly.

You may sit quietly.

Breathe slowly.

Repeat a sacred word softly.

And yet beneath the surface, something subtle begins changing.

The nervous system softens.

The inner pressure loosens.

The soul gradually releases its frantic need to “get somewhere.”

And in that releasing, a deeper awareness quietly emerges.

God was never absent.

Only hidden beneath noise, fear, striving, and distraction.

The writer even cautions against turning prayer into a kind of inner physical effort. You do not need to push your mind upward into the sky. You do not need to force spiritual sensations or imagine yourself climbing toward heaven.

Love itself already knows the way.

And this realization can become profoundly healing.

Especially for those who have spent much of life exhausted from trying to earn closeness with God through effort, certainty, performance, or spiritual achievement.

Contemplation invites another way.

A softer way.

A quieter way.

Not passive.

Not careless.

But deeply trusting.

Like floating rather than grasping.

Like resting rather than climbing.

Like opening the hands instead of tightening them.

Over time, prayer becomes less about reaching upward and more about awakening inwardly to the Presence already holding you.

Not far away.

Not hidden behind impossible distance.

But nearer than breath.

Nearer than thought.

Waiting patiently beneath all the movement of the mind.

## Breath Prayer

> Breath in: *You are already near*
>
> Breath out: *I rest in Your presence*
