## 30 — The Nearness That Cannot Be Measured

> *Love arrives before thought can travel.*

Many people begin the contemplative path carrying the quiet assumption that God must somehow be far away. Heaven feels distant. Holiness feels hidden somewhere beyond ordinary life, beyond the visible world, beyond the reach of the human heart. And because the mind imagines God as distant, it often responds by striving — reaching upward through effort, intensity, discipline, or spiritual ambition as though divine nearness must somehow be earned.

But the deeper contemplative tradition gently loosens this idea. God is not reached through physical distance. The soul does not discover Him by climbing upward through the imagination or straining itself toward heaven. Contemplative prayer is not about forcing the mind into spiritual heights. It is something quieter than that. Something simpler.

The ancient writer understood how naturally the human mind turns spiritual language into physical imagery. We hear words like “ascend,” “lift up,” or “go inward,” and immediately we begin imagining movement through space. Yet contemplation is not truly a journey across distance at all. It is a surrender into Presence.

The soul does not travel toward God the way the body travels from one place to another. Love moves differently than physical movement does. Desire reaches where thought cannot fully enter. The heart can rest in God while sitting quietly in an ordinary room, breathing softly in the middle of an ordinary day.

This is why the contemplatives often speak of heaven as mysteriously near. Not geographically near. But spiritually near.

For wherever genuine love awakens toward God, the soul already begins participating in that deeper reality. A quiet longing for God often carries the heart farther than restless striving ever could. The old writer says the nearest way to heaven is run not by footsteps, but by desire.

And this remains profoundly true. A single sincere turning of the heart may open more deeply than endless spiritual effort. A weary person whispering a quiet prayer through exhaustion may be nearer to God than someone desperately trying to manufacture spiritual intensity or mystical certainty.

Because contemplation is not about producing extraordinary experiences. It is about consenting to Love. Resting within mystery. Allowing yourself to be drawn gently beyond the constant need to control, define, explain, and achieve.

This is why contemplative prayer often feels surprisingly simple on the surface. Nothing dramatic may appear to happen outwardly. You may sit quietly in silence. You may breathe slowly. You may softly repeat a sacred word or simply remain still before God.

And yet beneath that stillness, something subtle begins changing. The nervous system softens. The inner pressure loosens.

The soul slowly releases its frantic need to “get somewhere.” And within that releasing, a deeper awareness quietly begins to emerge: God was never truly absent at all. Only hidden beneath the noise of striving, fear, distraction, and endless mental movement.

The ancient writer even cautions against turning prayer into another form of inner strain. You do not need to push your mind upward into the heavens. You do not need to force spiritual feelings or imagine yourself climbing toward God.

Love itself already knows the way. And this realization can become deeply healing, especially for those who have spent much of life exhausted from trying to earn closeness with God through effort, certainty, performance, or spiritual achievement.

Contemplation offers another way. A softer way. A quieter way. Not passive. Not careless. But deeply trusting.

Like floating instead of grasping. Like resting instead of climbing. Like opening your hands instead of tightening them.

Over time, prayer becomes less about reaching upward and more about awakening inwardly to the Presence already surrounding and sustaining you.

Not far away. Not hidden behind impossible distance. But nearer than breath. Nearer than thought. Waiting patiently beneath all the movement of the mind.

### Breath Prayer

> **Inhale:** *You are already near*
>
