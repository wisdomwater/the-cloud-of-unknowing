## 50 — Entering the Hidden Darkness

> *To receive the Infinite, the soul must loosen its grip on everything it can hold.*

When you feel yourself drawn toward the deeper life of prayer, there comes a moment when you must be willing to release even the things that once helped you find God.

This does not mean rejecting the world or despising the gifts of creation. It does not mean abandoning reason, wisdom, or the beauty of sacred teachings. Rather, it means recognizing that every image, every thought, every feeling, and every understanding can only carry you so far. Beyond a certain point, the soul must learn to rest in God rather than in its ideas about God.

The ancient writer invites us into a radical simplicity.

For a time, set aside what your senses can grasp. Let go of what your eyes see, what your ears hear, and what your mind continually analyzes. Release your attachment to memories of the past and your projections into the future. Lay down even your spiritual concepts and cherished certainties. Not because they are false, but because God is greater than all of them.

This is the movement into holy darkness.

It is a journey beyond the limits of ordinary knowing, where the soul no longer relies upon what it can understand. Here, love becomes the guide. Affection rises where thought can no longer climb. The heart reaches toward a mystery that the mind cannot fully contain.

At first, this can feel unsettling.

We are accustomed to building our lives around what we can perceive, explain, and control. We seek security in understanding. We want clear images, firm conclusions, and reliable answers. Yet the deeper contemplative tradition gently reminds us that God is not another object to be understood. God is the boundless reality in whom all understanding itself exists.

The closer we draw to the Divine Mystery, the more we discover that God transcends every category we create.

No image can contain Him.

No thought can define Him.

No doctrine, however beautiful, can exhaust the fullness of His being.

And so the soul enters a different kind of knowing.

Not a knowing that grasps, but a knowing that receives.

Not a knowing that possesses, but a knowing that participates.

Not a knowing through analysis, but through love.

The ancient writer warns that many people remain trapped within what they can comprehend. They assume that reality extends no further than the limits of their understanding. Some cling to ideas. Others cling to images. Still others create mental pictures of God and mistake those pictures for God Himself.

But the contemplative path asks something more.

It asks us to release our idols of certainty.

Even our finest concepts of God must eventually bow before the living Mystery they attempt to describe. Every image serves only as a doorway. Every teaching points beyond itself. Every sacred word is a finger pointing toward a moon it can never fully contain.

The goal is not ignorance.

The goal is humility.

For when the soul finally becomes willing to enter the darkness of unknowing, it discovers that the darkness is not empty at all. Hidden within it is a Presence more intimate than thought, more radiant than imagination, and more real than anything the mind can construct.

There, beyond the grasping of the intellect, the soul is quietly drawn upward by love.

And in that hidden darkness, God waits.
