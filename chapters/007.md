## 7 - Returning Before the Heart Hardens

> *Not every thought must become a home. Some things are healed simply by letting them pass.*

Thoughts are always moving through the mind. Memories, worries, desires, fears, and judgments arise without invitation, each asking for your attention. Contemplative prayer does not require you to stop these thoughts from appearing. It simply teaches you that you do not have to follow every thought that comes.

There is a difference between noticing a thought and attaching yourself to it. A feeling may surface, an old wound may ache, or a worry may begin speaking loudly within you. The invitation is not to fight these things or pretend they are not there, but to notice them with kindness and gently return your attention to God.

Many inner struggles grow because they are fed over time. Resentments are replayed, fears are rehearsed, and fantasies are nurtured until they begin shaping the atmosphere of the heart. Contemplation offers another way. Instead of becoming entangled, you learn to release what is passing through and remain rooted in something deeper.

This requires honesty and compassion. Many difficult thoughts arise from places of hurt, loneliness, exhaustion, or fear. You do not need to condemn yourself for having them. You can acknowledge them, hold them gently, and return again to the quiet presence of Love.

Over time, this simple practice changes you. The heart becomes less reactive, the mind less controlling, and the soul more at rest. Beneath the noise of thought, you discover a deeper stillness already held by God. Every gentle return becomes a reminder of where your true home is.

**Breath Prayer**

> Inhale: *Teach me gentle awareness*
>
> Exhale: *I return to Love*
