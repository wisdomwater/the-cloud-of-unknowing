## 19 — Letting Go of Yourself

> *The deepest surrender*
>
> *is not the loss of your humanity.*
>
> *It is the loosening of your grasp upon it.*

There comes a point in the contemplative journey where prayer begins moving beyond thoughts about God and into something far more intimate and unsettling:

the gradual releasing of yourself.

Not your existence.

Not your sacred worth.

Not the beautiful uniqueness of your soul.

But the constant inward clinging to yourself as the center of everything.

At first, much of spiritual life still revolves around the self. Your struggles. Your growth. Your understanding. Your healing. Your spiritual progress. This is natural. Human awareness begins from within our own experience.

But contemplation slowly draws the soul beyond this tight self-reference.

Little by little, the need to constantly monitor yourself begins softening.

You become less consumed with how you appear.

Less obsessed with managing every emotion.

Less entangled in endless self-analysis.

The soul grows quieter.

More transparent.

More spacious.

And in this quieting, something surprising happens:

you begin noticing how much inner suffering comes from clinging too tightly to your own identity, your own narratives, your own need for control and certainty.

The ancient contemplatives sometimes used strong language here because they were trying to describe how deeply the ego resists surrender. They speak about “forgetting yourself” or “hating yourself,” but beneath these phrases is not self-hatred in the modern sense. It is something gentler and deeper:

the loosening of self-centeredness.

The gradual willingness to stop placing your separate self at the center of every moment.

For the false self — the anxious, defended, endlessly grasping identity we construct — is constantly trying to secure itself through control, comparison, achievement, certainty, and recognition. It wants to preserve itself at all costs.

But contemplation gently exposes how exhausting this way of living truly is.

The soul begins longing for freedom from the endless burden of protecting and maintaining an identity.

And this longing can feel painful.

Because surrender always involves grief.

There are moments on the contemplative path where you become painfully aware of the distance between your restless inner world and the peace you long for in God. You see your fears more clearly. Your attachments. Your compulsions. Your endless mental noise.

At times, this awareness can feel overwhelming.

Not because you are failing spiritually.

But because you are finally seeing yourself more honestly.

The ancient writer describes this as a kind of deep sorrow — not despair, but a holy grief arising from the soul’s longing to rest fully in God while still feeling the weight of its own fragmentation.

This sorrow is important.

But it must be held gently.

For contemplation is never meant to become violence against yourself.

You are not called to crush your humanity.

You are not called to despise your body, your emotions, or your existence.

The contemplative path is not self-annihilation.

It is surrender into Love.

And real surrender always contains tenderness.

The ancient writer wisely warns against straining yourself too harshly. Even deep sorrow must unfold softly, without damaging the body or overwhelming the nervous system. There is a humility in allowing yourself to rest inside grief without turning it into punishment.

For beneath this sorrow, something beautiful is also growing:

desire.

A holy longing for union.

The soul begins discovering that no earthly identity can fully contain what it most deeply seeks. Beneath all the layers of personality and striving is a quieter yearning:

to belong completely to Love.

And strangely, the more this longing deepens, the more grateful the soul becomes simply for existence itself.

You no longer wish to escape being human.

You simply long to be freed from the illusion of separateness.

Freed from the constant tension of defending a false self.

Freed into communion.

Into presence.

Into God.

This path unfolds slowly.

Patiently.

And often through hidden inward transformations that cannot be forced or fully explained. There will still be struggle. Still moments of distraction, fear, resistance, and confusion.

But beneath all of it, Love continues its quiet work.

Softening.

Loosening.

Gathering the soul gently beyond itself.

## Breath Prayer

> Breath in: *Loosen my fearful grasp*
>
> Breath out: *Draw me into Love*
