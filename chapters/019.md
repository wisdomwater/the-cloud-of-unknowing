## 19 — Letting Go of Yourself

> *The deepest surrender is not the loss of your humanity. It is the loosening of your grasp upon it.*

There comes a point in the contemplative journey where prayer begins moving beyond thoughts about God and into something far more intimate and unsettling: the gradual releasing of yourself.

Not your existence. Not your sacred worth. Not the beautiful uniqueness of your soul.

But the constant inward clinging to yourself as the center of everything.

In the beginning, much of spiritual life naturally revolves around the self. Your struggles. Your healing. Your understanding. Your growth. Your spiritual progress. This is not wrong. Human awareness begins within the small world of our own experience. But contemplation slowly begins drawing the soul beyond this tight self-reference.

Little by little, the need to constantly monitor yourself begins to soften. You become less consumed with how you appear to others. Less entangled in endless self-analysis. Less exhausted by the constant effort of managing every emotion, securing every outcome, and protecting every fragile part of your identity.

The soul grows quieter. More transparent. More spacious.

And within this quieting, something surprising begins to emerge: you start noticing how much inner suffering comes from clinging too tightly to your own narratives, your own fears, your own need for certainty and control.

The ancient contemplatives sometimes used strong language when speaking about this surrender because they were trying to describe how deeply the ego resists letting go. They spoke of “forgetting yourself” or even “hating yourself,” but beneath these phrases is not self-hatred in the modern sense. It is something gentler and far more healing.

It is the loosening of self-centeredness.

The gradual willingness to stop placing your separate self at the center of every moment.

For the false self—the anxious, defended, endlessly grasping identity we construct—constantly tries to secure itself through comparison, achievement, control, recognition, and certainty. It works tirelessly to preserve itself. And contemplation slowly reveals how exhausting this way of living truly is.

The soul begins longing for freedom from the endless burden of maintaining an identity.

And this longing can feel painful.

Because surrender always carries some form of grief.

There are moments on the contemplative path when you become painfully aware of the distance between your restless inner world and the peace you long for in God. You begin seeing your fears more clearly. Your attachments. Your compulsions. The endless noise moving beneath the surface of the mind.

At times, this awareness can feel overwhelming. Not because you are failing spiritually. But because you are finally beginning to see yourself more honestly.

The ancient writer describes this as a kind of holy sorrow. Not despair, but a deep grief arising from the soul’s longing to rest fully in God while still feeling the weight of its own fragmentation. This sorrow matters. It softens the heart. It opens hidden places within us that pride often keeps sealed shut.

But it must always be held gently. For contemplation is never meant to become violence against yourself. You are not called to crush your humanity. You are not called to despise your body, your emotions, or your existence.

The contemplative path is not self-annihilation. It is surrender into Love. And real surrender always contains tenderness.

The ancient writer wisely warns against straining yourself too harshly. Even deep sorrow must unfold softly, without damaging the body or overwhelming the nervous system. There is humility in allowing yourself to rest inside grief without turning it into punishment. Love does not heal through cruelty.

And beneath this sorrow, something beautiful is quietly growing: desire.

A holy longing for union.

The soul slowly begins realizing that no earthly identity can fully contain what it most deeply seeks. Beneath all the striving, beneath all the self-protection and performance, there is a quieter yearning:

to belong completely to Love.

And strangely, the deeper this longing grows, the more grateful the soul becomes simply for existence itself. You no longer wish to escape being human. You simply long to be freed from the illusion of separateness. Freed from the endless tension of defending a false self. Freed into communion. Into presence. Into God.

This path unfolds slowly and patiently, often through hidden inward transformations that cannot be forced or fully explained. There will still be struggle. There will still be distraction, fear, resistance, and confusion. But beneath all of it, Love continues its quiet work within you.

Softening. Loosening. Gathering the soul gently beyond itself.

### Breath Prayer

> **Inhale:** *Loosen my fearful grasp*
>
> **Exhale:** *Draw me into Love*
