## 14 — Returning Beneath the Noise

> *The mind may wander endlessly. Love simply returns again and again.*

There are moments along the contemplative path when something opens unexpectedly within the soul. After long seasons of silence, surrender, confusion, or spiritual dryness, a sudden stillness may quietly appear. It is not always dramatic or emotional. Often it arrives gently, like soft light moving through morning fog, almost too subtle to describe.

For a brief moment, the noise falls away.

You become aware of a Presence deeper than thought itself. A tenderness beyond explanation. A quiet knowing that cannot fully be spoken aloud because language begins to grow fragile near mystery. The ancient contemplatives understood this well. They spoke carefully about such moments because they knew these experiences cannot be forced, controlled, or recreated by effort alone.

They come the way grace always comes: freely. Gently. Unexpectedly.

And because of this, contemplation slowly teaches humility. You begin to realize that the deepest movements of prayer are not achievements you manufacture through discipline, intelligence, or spiritual effort. They are gifts. Small unveilings of the Love that has been surrounding you all along.

Your task is not to force these moments into existence.

Your task is simply to remain available.

To return.

Again and again, you return with sincerity. You place your attention gently toward God even when nothing seems to happen. And this returning matters far more than you may realize.

For much of contemplative prayer appears very ordinary from the outside. You sit in silence. Thoughts arise. Distractions pull at your attention. Old memories surface unexpectedly. Restlessness presses against the stillness you are trying to enter. At times it may even feel as though nothing within you is changing at all.

But beneath the surface, something patient is unfolding.

The roots of the soul are slowly growing deeper.

And often this deepening happens through repetition more than intensity. You return after distraction. You return after discouragement. You return after failure. You return after old wounds rise up yet again. Not harshly. Not with self-condemnation. But with quiet faithfulness.

The contemplative life is not about becoming someone who never struggles with thoughts, emotions, weakness, or fear. It is about learning not to remain completely captive to them. Thoughts will continue appearing. Memories will continue surfacing. The mind will continue searching for certainty, control, distraction, and escape. This is simply part of being human.

But slowly, you begin discovering that you do not have to follow every thought that enters the mind. You can allow many things to pass beneath the cloud of forgetting for a little while. You can stop feeding every fear with endless attention and endless analysis.

And in this gentle releasing, space begins to appear. Space for stillness. Space for love. Space for God.

At first, this work can feel tiring and difficult because the mind has spent years learning constant movement. Many people become discouraged when they first encounter how noisy and restless their inner world truly is. But this discovery is not failure.

It is awakening.

You are finally beginning to see what has always been happening beneath the surface of your life. And little by little, through patient practice, silence becomes less frightening. The heart learns that it can survive without constant stimulation. The nervous system begins softening. You become less reactive to every passing emotion, every anxious thought, every storm moving through the mind.

Not perfectly.

But gradually.

And along the way, another beautiful thing quietly begins unfolding within you: compassion.

For once you begin seeing your own restlessness clearly, it becomes harder to judge others harshly. You begin recognizing how much hidden struggle exists within every human being. The contemplative path softens the instinct to condemn because you become more aware of your own need for mercy.

So you stop spending so much energy measuring who is holy and who is not. Who deserves grace and who does not. You leave more room for mystery. More room for healing. More room for the possibility that God may be working invisibly within people you do not yet understand.

And all the while, beneath the noise, beneath the thoughts, beneath the endless movements of the mind, Love continues waiting for you patiently.

Not demanding perfection.

Only willingness.

### Breath Prayer

> **Inhale:** *I return again to You*
>
> **Exhale:** *Beneath the noise within me*
