## 66 — Beyond Images Into Mystery

> *Love carries the soul where thought can no longer follow.*

The contemplative path gradually teaches us to see differently. In the beginning, we approach God through words, ideas, images, and reflections. These are valuable gifts. They help awaken faith and direct the heart toward what is good and true. Yet there comes a point when the soul discovers that God is greater than every image it can hold and deeper than every thought it can think.

The ancient writer calls this discovery the "cloud of unknowing." It is not a cloud that hides God from us. Rather, it is the realization that God can never be reduced to anything our minds can fully grasp. As the soul gently releases its attachment to created things, even to its cherished ideas about God, it begins to perceive a deeper reality. It sees that God is not this object, not that image, not this concept, not that definition. One by one, all limited understandings fall away.

What remains is not emptiness.

It is mystery.

The soul discovers a Presence beyond description. It cannot explain what God is, yet it knows with growing certainty what God is not. The old images no longer satisfy because they have served their purpose. They pointed toward the Divine, but they were never the Divine itself. In letting them go, the heart becomes available to a deeper encounter.

This is why the author speaks both of the "cloud of forgetting" and the "cloud of unknowing." The cloud of forgetting is the gentle laying aside of all distractions, attachments, images, and preoccupations. The cloud of unknowing is what remains when those things have been released. One is an act of surrender; the other is the sacred space that surrender creates.

Yet this movement is not accomplished through human effort alone. The soul certainly participates, but grace always leads the way. The deeper movements of contemplative prayer arise from God's own invitation. Love begins to flow from a source deeper than personal preference or spiritual ambition. The soul learns to follow rather than direct, to receive rather than control.

The author compares this process to a teacher guiding the hand of a child learning to write. The child is genuinely writing, yet the teacher's hand quietly supports and directs the movement. So it is in contemplative prayer. We remain active participants, but we gradually discover that God has been the deeper source of every movement toward Him all along.

This is why spiritual growth cannot be rushed. The contemplative life is not built upon techniques but upon transformation. Before the soul can live in this simplicity, it must first be shaped through faithfulness, prayer, repentance, surrender, and love. The roots must grow deep before the tree can bear fruit.

And even then, every soul is led differently.

Some experience gradual change over many years. Others encounter unexpected moments of profound grace. Some spend most of their lives in simple forms of prayer and become deeply holy there. None of these paths are inferior. God is not measuring the height of our experiences but the openness of our hearts.

One of the clearest signs of maturity along this path is a growing purity of intention. In the beginning, much of our spiritual life is mixed with self-interest. We seek comfort, certainty, healing, understanding, or peace. These desires are natural and often necessary. Yet over time, love slowly becomes less focused on what it receives and more focused on the One it loves.

The contemplative heart learns to choose God simply because God is worthy of love.

Not because God gives gifts.

Not because God solves problems.

Not because God provides spiritual experiences.

But because God is God.

This choice need not be dramatic. It is often quiet and hidden. Again and again, the soul turns toward God for His own sake. Again and again, it releases lesser motives and returns to simple love. This movement is already present within every genuine aspiration of the heart.

And as love becomes purer, the cloud itself begins to feel less like darkness and more like home.
