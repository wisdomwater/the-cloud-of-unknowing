## 32 — Beneath the Noise of the Mind

> *Not every thought speaks truth.*
>
> *Not every feeling leads toward life.*
>
> *Grace teaches the soul how to become quiet again.*

One of the hardest discoveries on the contemplative path is realizing how restless the mind can be.

Even when you sincerely desire God.

Even when you long for peace.

Even when you sit down in prayer with honest intention.

Thoughts continue moving.

Images appear without invitation.

Old memories surface.

Anxieties repeat themselves.

The imagination wanders endlessly between fear, fantasy, desire, regret, and distraction.

The ancient contemplatives understood this deeply. They did not describe the wandering mind with shame or harshness, but with honesty. Part of the human condition is this inward fragmentation — the feeling that different parts of us are constantly pulling in different directions at once.

One part longs for stillness.

Another seeks comfort.

Another searches for certainty.

Another wants distraction from pain.

And because of this inner movement, prayer can sometimes feel less like entering peace and more like becoming painfully aware of how noisy the inner world has become.

But this awareness is not failure.

It is the beginning of seeing clearly.

The writer describes imagination as a beautiful power that has become unruly. It continually creates images, stories, fears, desires, and interpretations. Sometimes it even tries to turn spiritual realities into mental pictures the soul can control.

This is why contemplation gently leads you beyond imagination.

Not because imagination is evil.

But because God is larger than anything the mind can construct.

The mind constantly tries to make mystery manageable.

To reduce the Infinite into concepts small enough to grasp.

And yet the deeper Presence of God can never be fully contained inside mental images, emotional intensity, or spiritual fantasies.

This can feel unsettling at first.

Especially for people who have relied upon certainty, explanations, or emotional reassurance to feel spiritually safe.

But contemplation slowly teaches another way of relating to the mind.

You do not have to fight every thought.

You do not have to panic over distraction.

You do not have to force your inner world into perfect silence.

Instead, you gently return.

Again and again.

Like placing a trembling hand back into the hand of God.

Over time, the soul learns that peace is not created through domination of the mind, but through surrender beneath the noise of it.

The writer also speaks honestly about desire and the senses. Human beings naturally move toward comfort and away from discomfort. We seek what feels pleasurable. We resist what feels painful. Much of ordinary life is shaped by these invisible movements beneath the surface.

And yet without awareness, these impulses can quietly rule us.

Not only physically.

But emotionally and spiritually.

We grasp for reassurance.

For distraction.

For stimulation.

For control.

For anything that temporarily softens the ache of uncertainty within us.

But contemplation invites a gentler freedom.

Not the destruction of desire.

The healing of desire.

The gradual reordering of the heart toward what truly gives life.

And this healing unfolds slowly through grace.

Not through self-hatred.

Not through harsh discipline.

Not through spiritual perfectionism.

But through patient returning.

Patient honesty.

Patient surrender.

The writer says there are moments in contemplation when the soul rests above itself and beneath God — beyond ordinary thought, beyond images, beyond analysis — resting only in simple loving awareness.

Not possessing God.

Not fully understanding God.

Simply resting in Presence.

And in those moments, something within the soul begins softening into union.

Not that you become God by nature.

But that Love draws you into deep communion with the One who has always held you.

This is the quiet mystery hidden beneath contemplative prayer.

Not escape from being human.

But the gradual healing of the divided heart.

The slow return from noise into wholeness.

The rediscovery that beneath all the confusion, fear, striving, and distraction, the soul still carries a deep longing for God.

And God has never stopped gently drawing it home.

## Breath Prayer

> Breath in: *Quiet the noise within me*
>
> Breath out: *Draw me into Your peace*
