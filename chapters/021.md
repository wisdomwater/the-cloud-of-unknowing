## 21 — The Quiet Depth of Prayer

> *God is found most deeply*
>
> *where the soul grows still.*
>
> *Love becomes clearer in silence.*

One of the quiet transformations that happens along the contemplative path is this:

Prayer slowly becomes less performative.

At first, many people approach God the same way they approach the rest of life — through effort, explanation, intensity, and outward expression. We try to say the right words. We search for the right feeling. We strain inwardly, hoping our sincerity will somehow bridge the distance between ourselves and the Divine.

But over time, something softer begins to emerge.

The soul starts realizing that God does not need to be persuaded of our longing. Love already sees us completely. Nothing is hidden. Nothing must be theatrically displayed in order to become known.

And because of this, prayer slowly becomes quieter.

Not empty.

Not distant.

But deeper.

The old contemplatives understood something many people still struggle to trust: God is not reached through emotional performance. The deepest communion happens beneath the surface noise of the self. Beneath the body’s tension. Beneath the mind’s constant need to grasp, explain, or secure certainty.

There is a form of prayer that is almost hidden even from yourself.

A simple inward turning.

A resting of attention in love.

A silent consent to God’s presence.

And strangely, this hiddenness often brings the soul nearer to God than dramatic displays of devotion ever could.

This does not mean the body is bad.

Far from it.

The body is holy.

Your tears are holy.

Your whispered prayers are holy.

Sometimes love overflows naturally into words, gestures, warmth, trembling, or deep emotional tenderness. Sometimes a person feels flooded with peace or comfort while praying. Sometimes the heart becomes so full that the lips softly whisper the name of Jesus without planning to.

There is nothing wrong with this.

The contemplative path does not reject the body. God meets us as whole beings — body and soul together.

But the ancient writer offers a gentle caution here: do not confuse spiritual depth with emotional intensity alone.

Not every powerful sensation is divine wisdom.

Not every unusual experience is sacred guidance.

The mind can become overwhelmed when it clings too tightly to spiritual feelings, visions, sounds, or extraordinary experiences. Fear, exhaustion, longing, imagination, and nervous-system strain can all mingle together in ways that become confusing.

This is why contemplation asks for groundedness.

Sobriety.

Patience.

The soul learns not to chase experiences, but to remain quietly anchored in love itself.

For genuine prayer does not pull you away from reality.

It softens you into greater honesty, humility, tenderness, and peace.

And this inner steadiness becomes a kind of quiet discernment.

When the heart rests deeply in God, it becomes less fascinated by spectacle. Less desperate for constant reassurance. Less vulnerable to every emotional wave or inward impression.

Love itself becomes the guide.

Not fear.

Not excitement.

Not spiritual ambition.

The ancient writer encourages the reader to stay rooted in simple contemplative love rather than becoming consumed with analyzing every spiritual sensation that appears along the way. This is deeply wise.

Many people become lost trying to interpret every feeling, every sign, every unusual moment. But contemplation invites something much simpler.

Remain near God.

Remain humble.

Remain soft.

If something is true, love will slowly clarify it in time.

And if confusion arises, you do not need to carry it alone. Seek grounded wisdom. Speak with trustworthy companions. Let humility protect you from the pressure of needing immediate certainty.

God is patient with your unfolding.

You do not need to force spiritual clarity before its season.

The deepest prayer is often far more ordinary than people expect.

Quiet attention.

Gentle surrender.

A heart resting silently in Love.

And in that hidden stillness, something within you slowly learns how to become transparent to God.

## Breath Prayer

> Breath in: *Quiet my restless striving*
>
> Breath out: *Teach me to rest in Love*
