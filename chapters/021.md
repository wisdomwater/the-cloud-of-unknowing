## 21 — The Quiet Depth of Prayer

> *God is often found most deeply in the still places of the soul.*

As the contemplative life deepens, prayer itself begins to change. What once felt driven by effort slowly becomes quieter, simpler, and more sincere. In the beginning, many of us approach God the same way we approach everything else in life — through striving, explanation, intensity, and the hope that if we can just say the right words or feel the right emotions, we might somehow draw nearer to the Divine.

But over time, something gentler begins to unfold within the heart.

The soul slowly realizes that God does not need to be convinced of our longing. Love already sees us fully. Nothing is hidden from the One who formed you. Nothing must be dramatically displayed in order to become known or received. And because of this, prayer gradually loses some of its performance and becomes more honest, more spacious, and more deeply rooted in presence.

Not empty. Not distant. But quietly deep.

The ancient contemplatives understood that communion with God does not arise from emotional intensity alone. Beneath the noise of the mind, beneath the tension of the body, beneath the constant need to grasp certainty or explain every experience, there is a deeper place where love simply rests in God without striving to possess Him.

Sometimes prayer becomes almost hidden even from yourself. It is no longer about producing feelings or reaching spiritual heights. It becomes a simple inward turning of the heart. A gentle resting of attention in divine love. A quiet consent to Presence.

And strangely, this hidden simplicity often brings the soul nearer to God than dramatic displays of devotion ever could.

This does not mean the body is unimportant. Far from it. The body is holy, and the emotions are not enemies of the spiritual life. Your tears are holy. Your whispered prayers are holy. There are moments when love overflows naturally into trembling, warmth, tenderness, silence, or the soft repetition of the name of Jesus arising from somewhere deeper than thought itself.

There is nothing wrong with this.

God meets us as whole beings — body and soul together.

Yet the contemplative path offers a gentle caution: do not mistake emotional intensity for spiritual maturity. Not every powerful sensation carries divine wisdom. Not every unusual experience is sacred guidance. The human mind can become overwhelmed when it clings too tightly to visions, signs, feelings, or extraordinary experiences. Longing, exhaustion, fear, imagination, and nervous strain can all become tangled together in ways that create confusion rather than clarity.

This is why contemplation teaches groundedness. Patience. Sobriety of heart.

The soul slowly learns not to chase experiences, but to remain quietly anchored in love itself. Genuine prayer does not pull you away from reality or inflate your sense of spiritual importance. Instead, it softens you into greater humility, honesty, tenderness, and peace. It makes you more present to others, more patient with yourself, and less controlled by fear or spiritual ambition.

As the heart grows steadier in God, it also becomes less fascinated by spectacle. It no longer needs constant reassurance or dramatic signs to feel secure. Love itself becomes the guide.

Not fear. Not excitement. Not the hunger to feel spiritually special.

The old contemplatives understood the wisdom of remaining rooted in simple love rather than endlessly analyzing every inward sensation that arises along the way. Many people lose themselves trying to interpret every feeling, every coincidence, every unusual moment. But the contemplative path invites something much quieter and far more trustworthy.

Remain near God. Remain humble. Remain soft. If something is true, love will clarify it in time.

And when confusion comes, you do not have to carry it alone. Seek grounded wisdom. Speak with trustworthy companions. Allow humility to protect you from the pressure of needing immediate certainty. God is patient with your unfolding. The soul does not ripen all at once.

In the end, the deepest prayer is often far more ordinary than people expect. It is quiet attention. Gentle surrender. A heart learning to rest silently in Love.

And within that hidden stillness, something in you slowly becomes transparent to God.

### Breath Prayer

> **Inhale:** *Quiet my restless striving*
>
> **Exhale:** *Teach me to rest in Love*
