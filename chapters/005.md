## 5 — A Simple Word for God

> *You do not need to carry many thoughts into prayer.*
>
> *Sometimes one small word*
>
> *is enough.*

As you begin walking more deeply into contemplative prayer, you may notice how easily the mind becomes tangled in thought.

Even beautiful thoughts.

Especially beautiful thoughts.

The mind may begin reflecting on the goodness of God, the tenderness of Christ, the sorrow of your past, the mysteries of suffering, the longing for healing, or the beauty of spiritual truth. And many of these reflections are sincere and holy. They may soften your heart. They may awaken devotion. They may even bring tears.

This kind of prayer has its place.

The soul often needs these meditations for a long time.

They help awaken love.

They help loosen the heart.

They teach us to turn toward God with honesty and tenderness.

But eventually, contemplation invites you somewhere quieter.

Not away from love.

Deeper into it.

For there comes a moment when even good thoughts can become too crowded. The mind keeps reaching for more understanding, more images, more emotional reassurance. It wants to keep touching and examining every spiritual experience.

And gently, contemplation asks you to let your hands rest.

To stop pulling every mystery apart.

To stop chasing every thought that appears inside the silence.

This does not mean thoughts are evil.

It simply means they are not the destination.

Sometimes the mind can become so fascinated with spiritual ideas that it never actually settles into the living presence of God.

It circles endlessly around the doorway without entering the room.

And so when thoughts arise during prayer—even holy ones—you do not need to fight them harshly or become afraid of them. You simply do not follow them too far.

You let them pass beneath you like clouds drifting across an open sky.

And then you return again.

Quietly.

Lovingly.

Without strain.

The ancient contemplatives often practiced this returning with a single simple word.

A small word held gently in the heart.

Perhaps “God.”

Perhaps “Love.”

Perhaps another sacred word that draws your attention inward toward Presence.

Not as a magical formula.

Not as something repeated mechanically.

But as a soft anchor for the soul.

A way of consenting again to God when the mind becomes scattered.

You may discover that the shorter and simpler the word becomes, the more spacious prayer begins to feel.

Because contemplation is not about collecting spiritual ideas.

It is about resting your attention in love.

And this small word can become like a quiet doorway.

When distraction comes, you return to it.

When anxiety rises, you return to it.

When curiosity tries pulling you into endless analysis, you return again.

Not forcefully.

Not anxiously.

Just faithfully.

Over time, this simple returning begins reshaping something within you.

The mind slowly loses its need to control every experience.

The nervous system softens its constant vigilance.

The heart learns that silence is not abandonment.

And beneath the surface of ordinary awareness, love begins gathering the scattered parts of you back together.

There may still be wandering thoughts.

There may still be restlessness.

There may still be seasons where prayer feels dry or hidden.

But the work is still unfolding quietly beneath what you can measure.

You are learning how to remain with God without needing to possess God.

This is a deeper intimacy than the mind first understands.

And perhaps this is why contemplation so often feels small from the outside.

Nothing dramatic may appear to happen.

You simply sit.

You return.

You offer one small word into the silence.

And yet within that hidden faithfulness, the soul slowly learns how to abide in Love itself.

## Breath Prayer

> Breath in: *God of quiet love*
>
> Breath out: *Gather me into You*
