## 47 — Beyond Knowing Into Love

> *The mind may point the way, but love alone can enter the mystery.*

There comes a point on the spiritual journey when thinking can take you no further.

The reflections, meditations, and sacred ideas that once nourished your faith are valuable gifts. They help awaken the heart. They turn the soul away from distraction and toward God. Through them, grace gently prepares the ground. Yet there comes a season when God invites you beyond thoughts about Him into a more direct awareness of His presence.

This invitation is subtle.

You may begin to notice that the old ways of approaching prayer no longer satisfy as they once did. It is not that they were wrong. They have simply carried you as far as they were meant to carry you. What once served as a doorway now opens into a larger room.

The ancient writer reminds us that Christ Himself pointed toward this movement. During His earthly life, His followers loved His visible presence. They listened to His voice, walked beside Him, and found comfort in seeing Him with their own eyes. Yet Christ told them that it was for their good that He would depart. Only then could they discover a deeper intimacy—one not dependent upon physical sight, but rooted in the living presence of God within.

The contemplative path follows a similar pattern.

At first, we rely heavily on images, ideas, words, and reflections. They help us focus our attention and awaken devotion. But eventually God invites the soul into a simpler way of being. Instead of continually thinking about God, we begin learning how to rest in God.

This shift often feels uncomfortable.

The mind wants something to hold. It wants concepts to examine, questions to answer, and experiences to analyze. When those familiar supports are removed, prayer can feel empty or even frustrating. The intellect complains that it has nothing to do. The imagination searches for something to grasp. Yet this very emptiness becomes the doorway through which a deeper grace may enter.

Allow the mind to grow quiet.

Not because knowledge is bad, but because there are dimensions of divine love that cannot be reached through knowledge alone. The deepest realities of God are not solved like problems. They are received like gifts.

The ancient writer offers a striking reminder: knowledge often inflates the ego, while love builds up the soul.

This does not mean we abandon wisdom or learning. Rather, we learn their proper place. Knowledge helps illuminate the path, but love walks it. Knowledge can describe the fire, but only love can feel its warmth. The soul is transformed not merely by understanding God, but by surrendering to God's presence.

For this reason, seek feeling more than mastery.

Not emotional excitement or dramatic experiences, but the quiet, humble awareness that comes when the heart rests gently before God. Such prayer may seem simple. It may even seem unproductive. Yet beneath the surface, profound transformation is taking place.

You may protest that this prayer feels more like struggle than rest.

Perhaps you sit in silence and discover only distraction, resistance, and longing. One part of you wants to stay present, while another wants to flee toward easier comforts. You long to feel God more clearly, yet remain painfully aware of your own limitations. The experience can feel less like peace and more like a battle.

This is natural.

The rest spoken of by the contemplatives is not the absence of effort. It is the absence of confusion. Though the soul may struggle, it is no longer scattered among countless directions. It has chosen its orientation. It has turned toward God. Even in difficulty, there is a hidden simplicity in that choice.

And over time, what first feels painful becomes familiar.

The soul gradually discovers a sweetness hidden within surrender itself. Not because every prayer becomes easy, but because the heart learns to dwell more naturally in God's presence. The struggle remains, yet it is held within a deeper peace.

So continue with humility.

Continue with longing.

Continue with trust.

This work begins here, but it does not end here. Every moment of sincere turning toward God becomes part of an eternal movement of love. What starts as a fragile offering of attention slowly becomes a life rooted in communion.

And through it all, grace remains the true teacher.

The soul learns not by force, but by love.

### Breath Prayer

> **Inhale:** *Teach me beyond knowing*
>
> **Exhale:** *Lead me into love*
