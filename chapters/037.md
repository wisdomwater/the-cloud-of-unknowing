## 37 — The Simple Knowing That You Are

> *The soul comes nearest to God when it stops trying to become more than it already is before Him.*

There is a kind of spiritual exhaustion that comes from endlessly examining yourself. The mind searches through every wound, every failure, every hidden motive, hoping that somewhere within all the analysis it will finally discover peace. But the contemplative path eventually leads somewhere much quieter. Beneath all the restless thinking about who you are lies a simpler and more sacred truth: you are.

This small awareness is closer to God than you may realize.

You do not need great learning to notice your own existence. Even the simplest person knows inwardly that they are alive, present, aware. And this humble awareness becomes the beginning of contemplation. Not endlessly asking what you are, whether holy or broken, worthy or unworthy, but simply resting in the fact that you exist before God at all.

There is a time for honest repentance and self-knowledge. There is a time for seeing clearly the ways sin has wounded the soul and distorted the heart. But there also comes a moment when endlessly turning over your brokenness no longer leads to healing. The old writer speaks almost tenderly here, saying in effect: enough now. Let the filth go. Do not keep stirring through it. A soul can become trapped in self-preoccupation, even through spiritual self-examination.

Instead, turn gently toward God as you are.

Do not wait until you feel pure enough to come into His presence. Bring your weary and unfinished self directly into the mercy of divine love. Lay your soul before God the way a sick person lays a wound before a healer. The healing does not come because you fully understand yourself. It comes because Love touches you.

The contemplative life is not about perfect spiritual thoughts. It is about allowing your whole being to quietly lean toward God. Again and again, the mind will try to complicate this. Your thoughts will search for techniques, explanations, dramatic experiences, or spiritual accomplishments. Part of you will feel uncomfortable with how simple this path really is. The ego often distrusts simplicity because simplicity leaves no room for performance.

But this naked turning toward God carries the soul farther than restless spiritual striving ever can.

There are many beautiful forms of prayer and meditation, and each has its place. Thoughtful reflection, study, worship, and sacred imagination can nourish the soul deeply. Yet there are moments when love must become simpler than thought. Moments when the soul stops scattering itself across countless ideas and instead rests wordlessly before the One who holds all things together.

And in that quiet resting, something within begins to soften into union.

You are no longer trying to force your way toward God.

You are allowing yourself to be gently gathered into the Love that has always held you.

### Breath Prayer

> **Inhale:** *I rest before You*
>
> **Exhale:** *As I am*
