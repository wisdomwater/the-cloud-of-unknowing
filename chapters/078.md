## 78 — Holding Lightly, Loving Deeply

> *The soul finds freedom when it uses the world without trying to possess it.*

One of the great challenges of the spiritual life is learning how to live among created things without becoming trapped by them. We need food, rest, work, relationships, possessions, and responsibilities. We cannot simply withdraw from life. Yet we are also called not to lose ourselves within it.

The author offers a surprisingly gentle approach.

He does not suggest becoming obsessed with every small mistake. He does not recommend endless self-monitoring or constant anxiety about whether we are doing everything perfectly. Instead, he invites the soul to remain steadily turned toward God while holding all other things lightly.

The problem is rarely the creature itself.

The problem is attachment.

Food is not the obstacle.

Possessiveness is the obstacle.

Rest is not the obstacle.

Clinging is the obstacle.

The soul becomes burdened not by using the gifts of life, but by mistaking them for its final home.

When the heart is firmly rooted in God, created things begin to assume their proper place. We receive them with gratitude. We use them when needed. We release them when necessary. We neither worship them nor fear them.

The author even suggests that people who are sincerely seeking God need not become consumed with worry over every small imperfection. If, through weakness or forgetfulness, they occasionally take more comfort than necessary, they need not spiral into self-condemnation. They can acknowledge the mistake, learn from it, and continue their journey.

This is an important spiritual insight.

Love grows through returning.

Not through obsessing.

Many sincere seekers spend enormous energy analyzing every fault, every failure, and every misstep. Yet excessive self-focus can become another form of distraction. The contemplative path invites a different response: acknowledge what happened, receive grace, and continue walking toward God.

The eyes remain forward.

The heart remains open.

Love continues its work.

The author contrasts this freedom with the life of a person whose deepest concern remains self-satisfaction. When the self remains at the center, even ordinary activities become distorted. Food, possessions, recognition, comfort, and success gradually become objects of attachment. The soul begins arranging life around its own preferences.

But when God becomes the center, everything changes.

The same activities remain.

The same responsibilities remain.

Yet the orientation of the heart becomes different.

A meal becomes nourishment rather than obsession.

Possessions become tools rather than treasures.

Comfort becomes a gift rather than a necessity.

The soul learns to pass through life without clinging to it.

The author then returns to one of the deepest themes of the contemplative tradition: freedom from self-preoccupation. He speaks of losing the feeling of one's own being, language that can sound unusual to modern ears. Yet beneath the medieval terminology lies a profound insight.

Much of our suffering comes from being trapped inside a constant awareness of ourselves.

Our worries.

Our image.

Our comfort.

Our desires.

Our fears.

The contemplative path slowly loosens this grip.

As love deepens, the soul becomes less fascinated with itself and more absorbed in God. This does not erase individuality. It does not destroy personality. Rather, it frees us from carrying ourselves as the center of the universe.

For brief moments, the soul discovers what it is like simply to rest.

Not performing.

Not evaluating.

Not defending.

Not striving.

Just resting in the Presence of God.

The author acknowledges that such experiences cannot be fully explained. They must be lived. There are dimensions of prayer that remain mysterious until they are encountered directly. The language points toward them, but cannot replace them.

This is why the contemplative tradition consistently returns to practice rather than theory.

Love.

Return.

Surrender.

Trust.

The path is walked, not mastered.

Finally, the author offers a warning that feels remarkably relevant today. Spiritual growth does not come through overthinking everything. Excessive analysis, endless spiritual self-evaluation, and chasing emotional experiences can all become obstacles.

The safer path is often the simpler path.

A quiet turning of the heart.

A steady desire for God.

A willingness to follow grace without forcing experiences.

The soul does not reach God by becoming more complicated.

It reaches God by becoming more available.

And availability begins when we learn to hold everything lightly except love itself.

### Breath Prayer

> **Inhale:** *Help me hold lightly*
>
> **Exhale:** *And love deeply*
