## 9 — Love Draws You Higher Than Shame

> *The soul is not healed by endlessly staring at its darkness. It is healed by turning toward Love.*

There is a kind of spiritual life built almost entirely upon self-condemnation. A person becomes consumed with their failures, weaknesses, mistakes, and unworthiness. They look inward constantly, searching for flaws, measuring their progress, rehearsing old regrets again and again. At first this can appear humble, but often it is simply another form of being trapped within the self.

True humility is gentler than this.

It does not deny your wounds or pretend you have never harmed yourself or others. Honest sorrow and repentance are real parts of the spiritual path. There are seasons when the soul must sit honestly with its brokenness and allow grief to soften the heart. But contemplation slowly teaches something deeper: you are not meant to remain forever bent over the memory of your sin.

At some point, love asks you to lift your gaze again.

Many people secretly believe they become holy by thinking constantly about how unworthy they are. Yet the saints did not become radiant because they were endlessly fascinated with their failures. They became radiant because they were captivated by God. The deeper contemplative path is not built upon obsession with spiritual inadequacy. It is built upon longing—a longing for union, a longing to remain near the Divine, a longing that slowly becomes stronger than shame itself.

And strangely, this longing creates a deeper humility than self-condemnation ever could.

For when the soul truly encounters the vastness of divine love, illusions of self-importance begin falling away naturally. Not violently. Not through humiliation. But through awe. The heart realizes it does not sustain itself. It is being sustained. It realizes grace was never earned. It was always gift.

And this realization softens the soul more deeply than fear ever can.

You begin understanding that humility is not thinking less of yourself because you are worthless. It is resting in the truth that everything is held by Love. Even you. Especially you.

This is why contemplation invites you to stop endlessly digging through the debris of your past searching for healing through self-analysis alone. The mind can become trapped there for years—replaying failures, rehearsing shame, searching every memory as though freedom might finally arrive through perfect self-understanding. But healing rarely comes through endless inner excavation.

Healing comes through turning. Turning again toward God. Turning again toward love. Turning again toward the quiet Presence already waiting beneath your fear.

The contemplative path does not ask you to deny your humanity. It simply asks you not to remain imprisoned within it. There are wounds that require honesty, sins that require repentance, and patterns that need gentle awareness and transformation. But once these things are acknowledged sincerely, the soul must not remain staring downward forever.

Love is always calling you further inward and upward. Not away from truth. Into deeper truth.

For the deepest truth is not merely that you are wounded. It is that God still desires union with you. And this longing of God toward the soul changes everything. You begin discovering that spiritual transformation happens less through anxious striving and more through sustained openness to grace—like sunlight slowly warming frozen ground, like water patiently reshaping stone.

You sit before God. You bring your small love. You return after distraction. You allow yourself to be seen without hiding. And over time, the soul slowly becomes less defended, less afraid, and more able to receive.

This is why the contemplatives spoke so often of longing. Because love keeps drawing the soul beyond self-preoccupation and into communion. And the more deeply love awakens within you, the less you need shame to keep you close to God.

Love itself becomes enough.

### Breath Prayer

> **Inhale:** *Lift my heart toward Love*
>
> **Exhale:** *Beyond shame, into grace*
