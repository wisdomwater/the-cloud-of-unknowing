## 43 - The Cross of the Self

> *The deepest surrender is not the loss of life, but the loosening of self.*

As love deepens within you, there may come a season when your constant awareness of yourself begins to feel like a veil. This is not because your humanity is a problem to overcome, but because the heart has glimpsed a greater freedom. You begin to notice how often your attention circles back to your fears, desires, wounds, progress, and concerns. What once seemed natural now feels heavy, and a longing awakens to rest more fully in God.

This is part of the hidden cross of the contemplative life. You may sincerely desire to surrender, yet still find yourself pulled again and again into self-concern. The mind keeps returning to its familiar stories. The ego continues seeking control, certainty, and recognition. In this struggle, you discover how difficult it is to free yourself through effort alone.

Yet this realization is not a failure. It is the beginning of humility. Instead of fighting yourself with frustration, you learn to bring your restlessness before God with patience and honesty. Grace works gently within the very places you cannot heal on your own. Over time, what once felt like an unbearable burden begins to soften into trust.

As surrender deepens, moments of quiet stillness begin to appear. Not dramatic experiences or spiritual triumphs, but a simple awareness that God's presence is nearer than your striving. In these moments, you taste a peace that does not depend on having everything figured out. Love carries what your own strength cannot.

Along the way, meditation, reflection, and sacred imagination remain valuable companions. They help awaken the heart and turn it toward God. Yet eventually, love invites you beyond thoughts and images into simple presence. Through the tenderness of Christ, you are led into a deeper communion where the heart learns to rest, not in its own efforts, but in Divine Love itself.
