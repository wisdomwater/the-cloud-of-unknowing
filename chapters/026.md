## 26 — The Humility That Does Not Perform

> *True humility does not try*
>
> *to appear humble.*
>
> *It simply forgets itself in Love.*

One of the stranger temptations of the spiritual life is the temptation to appear spiritual.

The ego is remarkably adaptable. Even the longing for God can quietly become entangled with the desire to seem wise, holy, awakened, pure, or enlightened in the eyes of others. Sometimes people begin speaking differently, moving differently, or presenting themselves in carefully crafted ways that subtly ask to be recognized as “deeply spiritual.”

But the ancient contemplatives warn gently about this.

For real transformation rarely draws attention to itself.

It becomes quieter over time.

More natural.

More sincere.

A truly grounded soul does not need to constantly display humility because humility has already begun softening the need for self-display altogether.

This is why contemplative prayer often makes a person simpler.

Their spirituality becomes less theatrical.

Less strained.

Less concerned with appearance.

The ancient writer speaks sharply at moments here, but beneath the strong language is an important truth: spirituality can become performative when inward peace has not yet fully settled into the heart.

A person may learn the language of devotion while still carrying hidden anxiety within. They may adopt a spiritual tone, a spiritual appearance, even spiritual mannerisms — yet still remain deeply unsettled beneath the surface.

And this is not something to condemn.

It is something to notice compassionately.

Many people learned early in life that love must be earned through performance. Some learned to survive by appearing pleasing, impressive, moral, spiritual, wise, or emotionally composed. And without realizing it, these same survival patterns can quietly enter the spiritual life.

Prayer then becomes another stage upon which the self tries to secure belonging.

But contemplation slowly loosens this burden.

The soul no longer needs to appear holy in order to rest in God.

It no longer needs to curate itself constantly.

Love becomes less about image and more about honesty.

And honesty has a very different feeling to it.

It relaxes the body.

It softens the voice.

It removes the pressure to constantly manage how one is perceived.

The ancient contemplatives observed that genuine prayer gradually creates a kind of naturalness within a person. Their humility no longer feels exaggerated or self-conscious. Their words carry warmth without performance. Their presence becomes grounded rather than inflated.

This kind of sincerity is deeply healing.

You can feel it around certain people.

Nothing in them is trying too hard.

Nothing is demanding admiration.

They are not performing spirituality.

They are simply becoming present.

And often this presence carries more quiet wisdom than all the dramatic displays of religiosity in the world.

The contemplative path invites you into this same freedom.

You do not need to manufacture a spiritual identity.

You do not need to sound mystical.

You do not need to appear endlessly serene.

You do not need to convince others that you are close to God.

The soul grows healthiest when it no longer needs to constantly monitor how holy it appears.

Instead, attention slowly returns to the deeper work:

becoming honest,

becoming gentle,

becoming inwardly aligned with Love.

This kind of transformation often happens quietly and invisibly. It unfolds in hidden moments of surrender, compassion, humility, repentance, patience, silence, and trust.

And because it is real, it naturally begins shaping the outward life as well.

Not artificially.

Not dramatically.

But sincerely.

The body softens.

Speech becomes kinder.

Listening deepens.

Presence steadies.

The person becomes easier to be around because they are no longer carrying the exhausting tension of trying to become someone special.

This is the quiet beauty of contemplative prayer.

It slowly teaches the soul how to stop performing and simply rest in what is true.

And in that resting, something deeply human and deeply holy begins to emerge.

## Breath Prayer

> Breath in: *Free me from performance*
>
> Breath out: *Teach me honest Love*
