## 20 — The Gentle Way of Love

> *Love grows quietly. Not through force, but through softness and surrender.*

One of the great temptations along the spiritual path is the temptation to force what can only unfold gently.

When people first discover contemplative prayer, they often approach it with tremendous intensity. Their longing for God feels sincere and urgent. They desire healing, peace, awakening, transformation. And because the longing itself feels so strong, they sometimes assume that greater force must produce greater spiritual progress.

So they strain inwardly. They push themselves harshly. They try to manufacture spiritual depth through sheer effort.

But the ancient contemplatives speak very carefully about this danger. They understood that there is a profound difference between sincere longing and spiritual violence.

The nervous system can become overwhelmed when the soul tries to seize what can only be received. Emotional intensity, physical sensations, agitation, or psychological overwhelm may sometimes be mistaken for spiritual depth. And in certain seasons, people even begin confusing exhaustion, fantasy, or emotional frenzy with the presence of God.

This confusion can wound the soul deeply.

For the contemplative path is not meant to inflame the body or fracture the mind. It is not about producing dramatic experiences or forcing yourself into altered states of emotion. Love moves differently than that.

Quietly. Patiently. Like water softening stone over many years.

The ancient writer offers a beautiful piece of wisdom here: work more with willingness than with force.

This changes everything.

Contemplation is not accomplished through aggression toward yourself. It unfolds through consent. Through openness. Through a relaxed and sincere inward turning toward God. Not laziness. Not passivity. But gentle availability.

And this gentleness matters deeply because the ego often hides itself inside spiritual ambition. Part of us wants to become exceptional, advanced, spiritually impressive. We want reassurance that we are progressing. We want experiences that make us feel certain, special, or secure.

But contemplation slowly humbles these desires. God is not grasped through intensity. Love is not captured through striving.

The deepest prayer often feels surprisingly simple and quiet. Almost ordinary. The soul sits before God without demanding constant reassurance or dramatic feelings. It learns to wait without forcing. To desire without clutching. To remain open without collapsing into desperation.

This is why the ancient writer speaks so tenderly about “playfulness” with God.

Not childishness in an immature sense, but the relaxed trust of a child resting safely within loving presence. There is something profoundly healing about this image because so many people carry hidden fear into prayer. Fear of failure. Fear of rejection. Fear that they are not pure enough, disciplined enough, or spiritual enough. And because of this fear, they tighten inwardly. They approach God anxiously, trying to earn closeness through effort and intensity.

But contemplative prayer invites another way.

A softer way.

The soul no longer lunges desperately toward God like someone trying to seize approval. Instead, it becomes more patient. More receptive. More willing to let Love unfold in its own timing.

And strangely, it is often then that deeper intimacy begins appearing. Not when you are grasping hardest. But when you finally relax enough to receive.

The ancient writer even suggests “hiding” your desire from God in a playful way—not because God does not already know it, but because this gentle restraint helps move the soul beyond emotional agitation into deeper simplicity. The longing becomes quieter. Cleaner. Less dramatic. More rooted in trust.

And slowly, this inward simplicity gathers the soul into union. Not through emotional frenzy. Not through mystical performance. But through quiet harmony between your heart and God’s presence.

Like two notes slowly learning how to resonate together.

This is why contemplation requires tenderness toward yourself. Care for your body. Rest when needed. Move gently with your limitations. Do not confuse harshness with holiness.

The soul flowers best in an atmosphere of patience and mercy.

And beneath all your effort, beneath all your longing, beneath all your unfinished growth, God remains already nearer than your striving can reach. Waiting patiently. Inviting softly. Drawing you inward through Love itself.

### Breath Prayer

> **Inhale:** *Teach me gentle surrender*
>
> **Exhale:** *I rest in Your Love*
