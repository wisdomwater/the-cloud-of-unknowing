## 11 — The Love That Cannot Be Measured

> *Some forms of prayer disappear with time.*
>
> *But love remains.*
>
> *Love is already touching eternity.*

There is a quiet freedom that begins to grow in the soul when you stop needing everyone to understand your inner life.

Not everyone will understand your longing for silence.

Not everyone will understand why you keep returning to stillness, prayer, or inward attention when the world rewards constant activity and visible achievement.

And that is alright.

Many people speak only from the level of life they themselves have known. If they have never tasted deep stillness, they may not recognize its value. If they have never felt the ache for God beneath words and concepts, they may assume contemplation is laziness, avoidance, or passivity.

But often they are not being cruel.

They simply do not yet see what you are beginning to see.

And when you remember your own blindness in past seasons of life, compassion naturally softens the heart. You begin realizing how often human beings judge what they do not yet understand. How often fear, busyness, and uncertainty shape the way people respond to one another.

So the contemplative path slowly teaches gentleness.

Not superiority.

Not separation.

Gentleness.

You no longer feel such urgency to defend yourself or explain every sacred thing unfolding within you. Something deeper begins anchoring the soul. You become more willing to let your life quietly speak for itself.

And this quietness itself becomes part of the prayer.

The ancient story of Martha and Mary points toward this mystery with remarkable tenderness.

Martha was not wrong for serving.

Her work mattered.

Love often takes the shape of meals prepared, burdens carried, people cared for, responsibilities fulfilled. The spiritual life is not an escape from ordinary life. Compassion expresses itself through action every day.

And yet Mary sat listening.

Resting.

Receiving.

She gave herself fully to presence.

And when Martha became anxious about this, Jesus did not shame her. He spoke gently to her troubled heart. He reminded her that beneath all the necessary tasks of life, there is still one thing deeper than all the rest:

to remain turned toward God in love.

This is the hidden center beneath every spiritual practice.

Not achievement.

Not religious performance.

Not mastering spiritual ideas.

Love.

A quiet, inward resting of the heart in God.

And over time, the soul begins discovering that many outward forms of spirituality belong only to this passing life. There are seasons for effort, for learning, for serving, for grieving, for seeking understanding. These things matter deeply while we are here.

But eventually many of them fall away.

There comes a point where thought cannot carry you further.

Words become too small.

Explanations become too thin.

And the soul stands quietly before mystery with empty hands.

But love remains.

The longing that reaches toward God in silence is already participating in something eternal. Even now, beneath your confusion and unfinished growth, something timeless is quietly unfolding within you.

You do not have to force this open.

You do not have to manufacture spiritual experiences.

You simply return.

Again and again.

You sit before God with honesty.

You release the need to grasp everything with the mind.

You allow yourself to be loved beyond understanding.

And slowly, almost invisibly, the heart becomes simpler.

More spacious.

More peaceful.

Less divided within itself.

The deepest contemplative prayer is not about escaping the world.

It is about learning to remain rooted in Love beneath the noise of the world.

And this love — quiet, hidden, wordless — is the one thing that cannot be taken from you.

## Breath Prayer

> Breath in: *Teach me to rest in Love*
>
> Breath out: *Beyond all understanding*
