## 11 — The Love That Cannot Be Measured

> *Some forms of prayer disappear with time. Love alone remains.*

There is a quiet freedom that begins to awaken within the soul when you no longer need every person around you to understand your inner life. Not everyone will recognize your longing for silence. Not everyone will understand why you continue returning to stillness, prayer, or inward attention while the world rewards constant movement, visible success, and endless productivity. And over time, you begin realizing that this no longer troubles you as deeply as it once did.

Many people can only speak from the depth of life they themselves have known. If they have never tasted deep stillness, they may not yet recognize its beauty. If they have never felt the ache for God beneath words, ideas, and outward religion, contemplation may appear strange to them—perhaps passive, impractical, or unnecessary. But often this misunderstanding does not come from cruelty. More often, it comes from limitation, fear, or unfamiliarity with the inward life.

And when you remember your own blindness in earlier seasons, compassion slowly softens the heart. You begin to see how often human beings judge what they have not yet experienced for themselves. Fear shapes perception. Busyness narrows attention. Uncertainty causes people to cling tightly to what feels familiar and measurable. The contemplative path slowly teaches you not superiority, but gentleness.

You no longer feel the same urgency to defend every sacred thing unfolding within you. Something quieter and deeper begins anchoring the soul. You become more willing to let your life speak softly for itself rather than constantly explaining your inward journey. In time, even this quietness becomes part of the prayer.

The ancient story of Martha and Mary points toward this mystery with remarkable tenderness. Martha was not wrong for serving. Her work mattered deeply. Love often takes the shape of meals prepared, burdens carried, responsibilities fulfilled, and ordinary acts of care offered faithfully each day. The spiritual life is never an escape from human life. Compassion must eventually become visible in how we love others.

And yet Mary sat listening. Resting. Receiving. She gave herself fully to presence.

When Martha became anxious and overwhelmed, Jesus did not shame her. He spoke gently to her troubled heart. Beneath all the necessary tasks and responsibilities of life, He pointed toward something deeper still: the quiet turning of the heart toward God in love.

This is the hidden center beneath every true spiritual practice. Not achievement. Not religious performance. Not mastering ideas or collecting spiritual knowledge. Love.

A quiet inward resting of the heart in God.

Over time, the soul slowly discovers that many outward forms of spirituality belong only to this passing life. There are seasons for effort and learning, seasons for service and grief, seasons for searching and understanding. All of these matter deeply while we walk through this world. But eventually many things begin to fall away.

There comes a point where thought can no longer carry you further. Words become too small for what the heart has begun to sense. Explanations grow thin at the edge of mystery. And there the soul stands quietly before God with empty hands.

But love remains.

The longing that reaches toward God in silence is already participating in something eternal. Even now, beneath your unfinished growth, your questions, and your uncertainty, something timeless is quietly unfolding within you. You do not need to force this open. You do not need to manufacture spiritual experiences or strive endlessly to feel something extraordinary.

You simply return.

Again and again, you return with honesty. You sit quietly before God as you are. You loosen your grip on the need to understand everything with the mind. You allow yourself to be loved beyond explanation.

And slowly—almost invisibly—the heart becomes simpler. More spacious. More peaceful. Less divided within itself.

The deepest contemplative prayer is not an escape from the world. It is the slow learning of how to remain rooted in Love beneath the noise of the world.

And this love—quiet, hidden, wordless—is the one thing that cannot be taken from you.

### Breath Prayer

> **Inhale:** *Teach me to rest in Love*
>
> **Exhale:** *Beyond all understanding*
