## 6 — Love Beyond Understanding

> *There is a kind of knowing*
>
> *that only becomes possible*
>
> *when you stop trying to grasp.*

The mind is not your enemy.

Your ability to think, reflect, learn, and understand is part of the beauty of being human. The intellect can awaken compassion, deepen wisdom, and help you recognize truth where it appears in the world. There are seasons of spiritual life where reflection and meditation nourish the soul deeply.

But contemplation begins where control begins to loosen.

For there is a difference between thinking about God and resting in God.

And many sincere seekers spend years circling around the Divine through thought alone, never realizing there is a quieter doorway waiting beneath all the effort.

The mind naturally wants clarity.

It wants to organize the mystery.

It wants explanations that feel solid and reassuring.

Even in prayer, the mind may begin offering beautiful reflections about God, holiness, suffering, forgiveness, heaven, or love. Some of these thoughts may be deeply moving. Some may help soften the heart for a season.

But eventually, contemplation asks you to release even these.

Not because they are bad.

Because they are smaller than the One you seek.

Love alone can enter where thought eventually reaches its edge.

This is one of the great hidden invitations of the contemplative life.

You begin learning how to remain present before God without needing to mentally possess the experience.

Without needing to explain everything.

Without needing certainty to feel safe.

And this is not easy at first.

The mind often resists silence because silence feels vulnerable. Thoughts create movement. Analysis creates the feeling of control. Even spiritual thinking can become a way of protecting ourselves from direct encounter.

So when contemplation gently asks you to let go of even holy thoughts for a time, the nervous system may feel uncertain.

You may wonder:

“Am I doing enough?”

“Shouldn’t I be thinking about something sacred?”

“Why does this feel so empty?”

But this emptiness is not absence.

It is spaciousness.

The soul is learning how to rest without constantly filling the silence.

And slowly, almost imperceptibly, something within you begins changing.

The endless inner commentary softens.

The pressure to achieve spiritual certainty loosens.

You become less driven by the need to grasp and more willing simply to receive.

This is why the contemplative path has often been described as a movement from seeing to loving.

There are forms of prayer where you reflect deeply on Christ, on mercy, on your own wounds, on compassion for others, on the beauty of creation. These are beautiful and necessary movements of the soul.

But deeper contemplation becomes strangely simple.

You are no longer focused primarily on experiences, images, insights, or emotional intensity.

You are simply turned toward God.

Like a flower slowly opening toward sunlight.

Like still water reflecting the sky.

You do not climb into this awareness through force.

You consent to it.

And even that consent is grace.

For the deeper movement of contemplation is not ultimately your striving upward toward God.

It is God gently drawing you inward.

Drawing you beyond the constant activity of thought.

Beyond self-measurement.

Beyond spiritual performance.

Into quiet communion.

There may still be distractions.

There may still be wandering thoughts.

You may still find yourself returning over and over again to your small prayer word, your breath, your longing, your willingness.

This returning is not failure.

It is the path itself.

For every gentle return teaches the heart how to trust Love more than mental control.

And over time, you begin discovering something surprising:

God was never waiting for you to understand everything.

Only to remain available.

Only to stay open.

Only to let yourself be loved inside the mystery.

## Breath Prayer

> Breath in: *Beyond thought, You remain*
>
> Breath out: *I rest in Your presence*
