## 6 — Love Beyond Understanding

> *There is a kind of knowing that only becomes possible when you stop trying to grasp.*

The mind is not your enemy. Your ability to think, reflect, learn, and understand is part of the beauty of being human. Through the intellect, compassion can deepen, wisdom can grow, and truth can become more visible within the world around you. There are seasons of spiritual life where reflection and meditation nourish the soul deeply and help awaken the heart toward God. Yet contemplation begins where control slowly begins to loosen, for there is a difference between thinking about God and resting in God.

Many sincere seekers spend years circling around the Divine through thought alone, never realizing there is a quieter doorway waiting beneath all the effort. The mind naturally longs for clarity. It wants to organize mystery into something understandable and reassuring. Even in prayer, it may begin offering beautiful reflections about holiness, suffering, forgiveness, heaven, mercy, or love. Some of these thoughts are deeply moving and genuinely nourishing to the soul. But eventually, contemplation invites you to release even these thoughts for a time—not because they are bad, but because they are smaller than the One you seek. Love alone can enter where thought eventually reaches its edge.

This is one of the hidden invitations of the contemplative life: learning how to remain present before God without needing to mentally possess the experience. You are no longer trying to explain everything or force certainty in order to feel safe. And this is not easy at first. The mind often resists silence because silence feels vulnerable. Thoughts create movement, and analysis creates the feeling of control. Even spiritual thinking can quietly become a way of protecting ourselves from direct encounter.

So when contemplation gently asks you to loosen your grip on even holy thoughts for a little while, the nervous system may feel uncertain. Questions may rise within you: *Am I doing enough? Shouldn’t I be thinking about something sacred? Why does this feel so empty?* Yet this emptiness is not absence. It is spaciousness. The soul is slowly learning how to rest without constantly filling the silence.

Over time, almost imperceptibly, something within you begins to change. The endless inner commentary softens. The pressure to achieve spiritual certainty loosens. You become less driven by the need to grasp and more willing simply to receive. This is why the contemplative path has often been described as a movement from seeing to loving.

There are forms of prayer where you reflect deeply on Christ, on mercy, on your own wounds, on compassion for others, and on the beauty of creation. These are beautiful and necessary movements of the soul. But deeper contemplation becomes strangely simple. You are no longer focused primarily on experiences, images, insights, or emotional intensity. You are simply turned toward God, like a flower slowly opening toward sunlight or still water reflecting the sky. You do not climb into this awareness through force. You consent to it. And even that consent is grace.

For the deeper movement of contemplation is not ultimately your striving upward toward God. It is God gently drawing you inward—beyond the constant activity of thought, beyond self-measurement, beyond spiritual performance, and into quiet communion. There may still be distractions. There may still be wandering thoughts. You may still find yourself returning again and again to your small prayer word, your breath, your longing, your willingness. But this returning is not failure. It is the path itself, for every gentle return teaches the heart how to trust Love more than mental control.

And over time, you begin discovering something surprising: God was never waiting for you to understand everything. Only to remain available. Only to stay open. Only to let yourself be loved inside the mystery.

> **Inhale:** *Beyond thought, You remain*
