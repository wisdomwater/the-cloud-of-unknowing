## 12 — The Heart That Refuses Lesser Comforts

> *The soul begins to change*
>
> *when nothing less than God*
>
> *can finally satisfy its hunger.*

There comes a season in the contemplative life when the heart begins losing interest in lesser comforts.

Not because creation is bad.

Not because relationships, beauty, work, or ordinary joys no longer matter.

But because something deeper has awakened underneath them all.

A longing that cannot fully rest in anything temporary.

At first, this longing can feel confusing. You may try to soothe it with distractions, accomplishments, spiritual ideas, endless learning, or the approval of others. You may even try to fill it with religious activity itself.

But eventually the soul begins recognizing that nothing created can completely hold the ache it carries for God.

And this realization is both painful and beautiful.

Painful because many things that once satisfied you no longer do.

Beautiful because the heart is slowly being gathered toward what is eternal.

This is the love the ancient contemplatives spoke about so carefully. A love that keeps searching beneath every surface experience for the Presence hidden within all things.

A love that cannot settle.

A love that quietly weeps beside the empty tomb because only the Beloved will do.

The old stories of Mary Magdalene carry this tenderness so powerfully. Even when others tried to comfort her, her heart remained fixed on Christ alone. Not even the voices of angels could distract her from her longing.

This is not rejection of the world.

It is the soul becoming honest about what it most deeply desires.

And many people experience moments like this without fully understanding what is happening. They feel restless in the middle of success. Lonely in the middle of noise. Spiritually thirsty even while surrounded by information, entertainment, or achievement.

The contemplative path invites you not to run from this ache.

But to listen to it.

For beneath the ache is love itself.

A holy homesickness.

A quiet remembering that you were made for communion deeper than thought alone can provide.

And because this longing is so inward and hidden, others may not always understand it. They may think you are becoming too quiet, too detached, or too unconcerned with worldly success. Some may even judge the simplicity of your life or the hiddenness of your prayer.

But contemplation slowly teaches you not to build your life around defending yourself.

You do not need to stop your inward turning every time someone misunderstands it.

You do not need to constantly justify your longing for silence.

Love itself becomes your explanation.

And over time, something else begins changing as well:

trust.

Not naïve certainty.

Not denial of difficulty.

But a quieter trust that God is able to sustain what God calls.

The contemplative path often feels uncertain because it loosens the illusion of control. The soul begins releasing its endless attempts to secure itself completely before surrendering to love. And this can feel frightening to the anxious mind that wants guarantees before it rests.

But slowly you begin discovering that grace often arrives before you know how everything will unfold.

Strength appears for the moment that requires it.

Provision comes in unexpected ways.

Patience grows quietly inside hardship.

And even when life remains imperfect, you begin realizing you are being carried more than you once believed.

This does not mean abandoning wisdom or responsibility.

It means releasing the exhausting belief that your survival depends entirely upon your own grasping and striving.

The contemplative life gently loosens the clenched fist.

And in that loosening, humility begins to bloom.

Not humiliation.

Not self-hatred.

But a softer awareness of your smallness inside the vastness of God.

A willingness to rest beneath mystery instead of mastering it.

And strangely, this humility becomes peaceful.

Because when you no longer need to hold the whole world together, the soul can finally breathe again.

## Breath Prayer

> Breath in: *Nothing less than Love*
>
> Breath out: *Can satisfy my soul*
