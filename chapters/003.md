## 3 — Love Knows What Thought Cannot

> *The mind reaches outward.*
>
> *Love enters inward.*
>
> *God is found more deeply by longing than by certainty.*

I would have thee understand something beautiful about thy soul.

Within thee are two great powers.

One seeks to know.
One seeks to love.

The knowing power is good and holy in its proper place. By it thou learnest, discernest, rememberest, and beholdest the wonders of creation. Through it thou mayest study sacred writings, ponder wisdom, and speak of divine things.

And yet there comes a place upon the contemplative path where knowledge alone cannot carry thee further.

For God cannot be contained by thought.

The mind strains upward, trying to grasp what is infinite, but every idea eventually fails. Every image becomes too small. Every explanation leaves something untouched. The intellect may circle around the mystery of God endlessly and still remain standing outside the doorway.

But love enters where thought cannot go.

This is the great secret I would place gently into thy hands.

Though God remains beyond the reach of understanding, He is never beyond the reach of love. A single movement of sincere longing toward Him pierces farther than many complicated thoughts. One humble stirring of affection reaches into the cloud more deeply than restless analysis ever could.

And therefore do not become discouraged when contemplation feels dark to thee.

Many souls imagine they are failing because they cannot think clearly about God during prayer. Their minds wander. Their understanding feels empty. Silence stretches before them and they fear nothing holy is happening.

But I tell thee truly:

this hidden work often begins precisely there.

Not in clarity.
Not in mastery.

But in naked desire for God.

If thou canst rest, even briefly, with a simple inward turning toward the Divine—without demanding immediate feeling or understanding—then already thou hast begun the work of contemplation.

For love knows how to remain.

And this work itself is smaller and simpler than many imagine. It may happen in a moment no longer than a breath. A single inward movement toward God. A quiet yielding of the heart. A brief lifting of the soul beyond distraction and self-concern.

Such moments seem small to the world.

But heaven measures differently.

One sincere turning toward Love carries eternal weight.

And yet thou mayest now feel sorrow when hearing this. Thou mayest think of wasted years, distracted hours, forgotten prayers, restless living, opportunities missed. Thou mayest look backward and feel overwhelmed by how little attention thou hast given to God amid the noise of life.

Do not remain there long.

For despair also is a temptation.

Instead, bind thyself gently to Jesu through love and trust. What thou cannot repair alone, Love itself begins healing within thee. For Christ is not merely the judge of thy life, but also its healer. He stands not only at the end of time asking for account, but beside thee now, helping thee awaken.

Love joins what was separated.

This is why even thy smallest sincere prayer matters.

When thou turnest toward God, thou dost not walk alone. Thou art joined mysteriously to all who love Him—the quiet saints, the hidden souls, the great cloud of witnesses who have learned to rest in divine love before thee.

And slowly, through many small returnings, the heart becomes reordered.

The scattered will begins gathering itself toward one desire.
The restless soul grows quieter.
Time itself becomes more sacred.

Not because thou hast become perfect.

But because thou hast begun consenting to Love.

And this consent, however small it first appears, is already the beginning of transformation.

## Breath Prayer

> Breath in: *Love draws me inward*
>
> Breath out: *Beyond thought, into God*
