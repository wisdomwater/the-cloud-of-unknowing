## 3 — Love Knows What Thought Cannot

> *The mind reaches outward. Love enters inward.*

There are different ways the soul reaches toward God. Part of you longs to understand, to find clarity and meaning, to hold faith with some sense of certainty. This desire is not wrong. The mind is a beautiful gift. Through it you learn, reflect, discern, and glimpse the wisdom woven throughout creation.

And yet there comes a place along the contemplative path where understanding can no longer lead the way.

For God is greater than thought.

The mind reaches upward, searching for language large enough to contain the Infinite. It forms ideas, images, and explanations in hopes of drawing nearer to the Divine. But eventually every concept becomes too small. Every definition fades at the edges. The deeper mystery of God always remains beyond what the intellect can fully grasp.

And this is not failure.

It is invitation.

For while thought eventually reaches its limit, love continues onward. Love knows how to remain near what it cannot fully explain. It does not demand complete understanding before it draws close. It simply rests, listens, and consents to Presence.

This is one of the quiet truths contemplation slowly teaches the heart.

You do not need perfect understanding in order to draw near to God. You do not need brilliant insight or emotional certainty. You do not need to force spiritual experiences into being. Often all that is needed is a small inward turning—a gentle movement of love toward the Divine.

And this movement is usually far quieter than people expect.

You may sit in prayer and feel distracted. Your thoughts may wander endlessly. Silence may seem empty at first. You may wonder whether anything meaningful is happening beneath the surface at all. But do not rush away too quickly. The hidden work often begins there.

Not in mastery. Not in dramatic experiences. But in simple willingness.

A willingness to remain present before God without demanding immediate clarity. A willingness to return again after distraction. A willingness to sit beneath the cloud without trying to force it open. These small returnings may appear insignificant, but over time they begin changing something deep within you.

The scattered parts of the soul slowly gather together. The constant striving softens. Prayer becomes less about achieving something and more about consenting to Presence. Even brief moments begin to matter—a quiet breath offered toward God, a small pause in the middle of a hurried day, a moment of surrender instead of control.

Love measures differently.

And if sorrow rises within you as you begin noticing how distracted or restless your life has been, hold that sorrow gently. Do not turn it into despair. The contemplative path is not built upon shame for the past. It is built upon returning.

Again and again, returning.

For Christ does not meet you merely as judge or observer. Christ meets you as healer, companion, and quiet Presence already waiting beneath all your wandering. Even now, you are not beginning alone.

Others have walked this hidden path before you—quiet souls, unknown saints, tender hearts who slowly learned how to rest in divine love beyond certainty and explanation. Their lives form part of the great communion into which you are also being welcomed.

And each small turning toward Love becomes part of your transformation.

Not all at once.

But gently.

Like dawn slowly filling a darkened room.
