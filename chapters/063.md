## 63 — The Longing That Leads Home

> *Love does not climb toward God by understanding, but by desire.*

At the heart of *The Cloud of Unknowing* lies a remarkably simple invitation: turn the whole energy of your heart toward God. The author is less interested in teaching techniques than in describing a movement of love. Names may differ from one spiritual writer to another, but the reality remains the same. Whether they speak of aspirations, elevations, contemplative prayer, or a blind stirring of love, they are all pointing toward the soul's gradual awakening to a deeper communion with God.

The spiritual journey often begins with reflection, meditation, and deliberate acts of devotion. These are valuable gifts. They help train the attention, awaken love, and prepare the heart for deeper things. Yet the author believes there comes a season when the soul is invited beyond thinking about God into a simpler and more direct movement toward Him. The mind grows quieter. Images become less necessary. Love itself begins to take the lead.

This transition rarely happens all at once. Like learning to walk, the soul may take a few uncertain steps before returning to familiar supports. There may be seasons when prayer feels simple and wordless, followed by seasons when meditation again becomes necessary. This is not failure. It is part of the natural rhythm of spiritual growth. God is patient with the soul, and the soul learns patience with itself.

Over time, these movements of love become gentler and more natural. What once required effort begins to arise spontaneously. The heart finds itself turning toward God almost instinctively, the way a flower turns toward sunlight. Prayer becomes less about doing and more about consenting. Less about achieving and more about responding.

The author describes these movements as aspirations. The word itself suggests reaching upward, not through ambition but through longing. An aspiration is the soul's quiet hunger for God. It is the heart stretching beyond what is familiar, drawn by a beauty it cannot fully explain. It is the inward whisper that says, "There is more," and the willingness to follow that whisper wherever it leads.

These aspirations are precious because they carry within them both desire and promise. They are not yet the fullness of union, just as thirst is not yet the drinking of water. Yet even the thirst contains a kind of happiness, because it is directed toward the One who alone can satisfy it. The longing itself becomes a gift.

Sometimes there are moments when the soul's longing gives way to a deeper stillness. The striving falls quiet. Thoughts recede. For a brief time, the heart rests in a simple awareness of God's presence. Nothing needs to be achieved. Nothing needs to be understood. Love simply rests in Love.

Such moments rarely remain forever. The soul returns to ordinary awareness, and the longing awakens again. But this is not a loss. Each return teaches the heart to seek more deeply, trust more fully, and surrender more freely. The cycle of longing and fulfillment becomes part of the soul's education in love.

The contemplative life is not a straight ascent toward heaven. It is a continual turning of the heart toward the One who is already nearer than we imagine. Every aspiration, every longing, every quiet movement of love becomes a step homeward. And little by little, the soul discovers that what it has been seeking all along has also been seeking it.
